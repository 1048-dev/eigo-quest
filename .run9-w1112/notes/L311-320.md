### Run 9 review notes: L311-320 (world 11, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A... or b..." says, US praise, "Ding-dong!", "Give a thumbs-up.", lowercase after "Now,"). There are 57 patches in 10 lessons (every lesson has at least one change). There is one learning-item ID change: L319 vocab "senior student" -> "older student". The most common fixes are the "formula read aloud" comma chains in "Here's the point" ("Say I want to be, then a, then the job.", "Say join the, then the club."), the made-up tennis sound "Pok!", and "more tall / more fast" in L314, which models a classic learner error. Every new English sentence has 8 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. The touched lessons are 205-224 s long with 89-90% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L311 What Do You Want to Be?
- A: The interview talk is natural ("I want to be a teacher, like you!", "I want to be a cloud!"). In seg8, "Say I want to be, then a, then the job." is not teacher talk.
- B: The -er = person tip is correct and well paced. EN and JA match. The article point (a を わすれないで) is good, but the English form of it is hard to follow.
- C: The seg8 comma chain cannot be read naturally. "says er" in seg9 may sound like a hesitation, but it is the sound being taught (single flag).
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "Here's the point. Say a before the job. I want to be a teacher." (A+C).

#### L312 Because I Like Bread
- A: The bakery scene and Birimo's whisper ("And I can eat all the bread!") are natural and fun. "Because I like bread." as a separate sentence is normal in speech after a why question. "Why gets a because!" is playful but OK.
- B: The drop-e + -er tip is correct. Kiko's line has the JA さっかにも ("also a writer"), but the English has no "also", so the meaning does not match.
- C: Reads well. "Beep beep!" and "Push, push!" are easy.
- Decision: conversation.lines[4].ja さっかにも -> さっかに (objective, JA/EN mismatch).

#### L313 I Practice Every Day
- A: "Big sports hall" is British-leaning. The scene says "sports center". "Strong fist!" is not something a US teacher says. A guts pose is a "fist pump". The seg20 recap "You can say your dream, and I practice running every day." blends a description and a quote.
- B: The double-letter tip is fine for Stage 1. Same flag on "Strong fist!" (unclear meaning), and the seg8 "Say I practice, then swimming or running." formula. "みんな ゆうしょうだ！" for "You're all winners!" is loose but acceptable (single flag).
- C: The seg8 and seg20 lines are hard to phrase. "Fist pump!" is easy to say.
- Decision: seg0 "sports hall" -> "sports center", JA スポーツセンター (A+B). seg8 -> "I practice swimming. I practice running. Put every day at the end." (A+C). seg12 "Strong fist!" -> "Fist pump!" (A+B). seg20 -> "You can say your dream. You can say, I practice running every day." (A+C).

#### L314 When I'm Older
- A: "Dear me, ten years from now..." sounds like the exclamation "Dear me!". A letter to yourself starts "Dear future me". "And say more with E R" sounds like "talk more". "Faster means more fast", "Riko is even more tall" and "Taller means more tall" are not what a teacher would model.
- B: "more tall / more fast" is exactly the error Japanese learners make with comparatives, so the lesson should not model it. The two meanings of -er are taught clearly otherwise. The quiz needs a clue that does not say "taller": "tall, tall, tall!" works, and the answer line can model "taller than".
- C: "Dear me," gets the wrong intonation. The rest reads well ("Two Gs!", "Slow, like a turtle.").
- Decision: seg0 -> "Dear future me...", JA 「みらいの わたしへ…」 (A+C). seg1 -> "And E R can mean more." (A+B). seg8 -> "And E R means more. Fast, faster." (A+B). seg18 -> "Riko is tall, tall, tall!" (A+B). seg19 -> "Riko is taller than Haru.", JA follows (A+B).

#### L315 Dream Radio Interview
- A: The radio show is natural ("We're on the air!", "That's a nice dream. How about you, Haru?"). "I'm a radio." / "You can't be a radio! Beep, boop!" is a good joke.
- B: The review of -er (person/more) and -ed is clear. In seg9, the JA それとも「もっと」の いみ uses a question word in a statement, which is unnatural Japanese. Haru's JA サッカーせんしゅ。 drops なりたい, but it is a natural short answer (single flag, kept).
- C: Reads well.
- Decision: seg9 subtitleJa それとも「もっと」の いみ -> 「もっと」の いみも ある (objective, JA).

#### L316 What Club Do You Want to Join?
- A: The club talk is natural. "Pok! Pok!" is not an English sound word. "Say join the, then the club." is a formula chain.
- B: The -est tip with "the" is correct. In seg7, "Me too?" has no speaker, so it sounds like Kiko (the JA has ハルくん…「ぼくも？」).
- C: TTS will read "Pok" oddly. The seg8 chain cannot be read naturally. Without "asks", the voice cannot show the speaker change in seg7.
- Decision: seg4 "Pok! Pok!" -> "Pop! Pop!" (A+C). seg7 -> "Haru stands on his toes and asks, Me too?" (B+C). seg8 -> "Here's the point. Say the before the club. I want to join the tennis team." (A+C).

#### L317 In Junior High School
- A: The uniform scene and Birimo's "I'm three hundred years old!" are natural. "Happy birthday, Birimo!" comes from nowhere: the quiz only says he is 300. In seg8, "Say I want to, then an action. Study, make, be." is a formula chain. "Jump the highest." is a slightly odd command, but it is a TPR item ID (kept).
- B: "be" is not an action, so seg8 also teaches something slightly wrong. The lesson's own model sentences are clearer. The birthday line confuses the meaning (おたんじょうび has no basis).
- C: "Ist." in seg9 is a non-word that TTS will mangle. The seg8 list is choppy.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "I want to study English hard. I want to make many friends." (A+B+C). The subtitleJa keeps the formula, as in earlier blocks. seg9 drop "Ist." (A+C). The JA keeps イスト. seg19 -> "Wow, three hundred years old!", JA わあ、300さい！ すごい！ (A+B).

#### L318 The Biggest Club
- A: The after-school talk is natural ("even on the hottest days!", "Squeak, squeak!"). "Ding-dong! The bell just rang." is right for a school chime. "Then say how it feels." is a little loose but fine.
- B: The double-letter -est tip is correct. In seg3, the JA かばんを しまって means "put your bag away", but the EN is "Pack your bag!".
- C: Reads well. "Gulp, gulp. Ahh!" is fun.
- Decision: seg3 subtitleJa -> どうぐを かばんに しまって！ (objective, JA/EN mismatch).

#### L319 Club Trial Day
- A: "Senior student" for せんぱい is a Japanese-English calque. In the US a "senior" is a 12th grader, and kids say "older students" or "the big kids". The quiz says Kiko laughed at the drama club, but in the story it was Haru.
- B: Same flag on "senior student": it teaches a wrong meaning. The drop-e / y-to-i tips are correct. "Can I try it?" is fine as polite. "shuttle" vs US "birdie" is a single flag (kept).
- C: "Older student. A big kid in your club." reads easily. The rest is fine ("Knock, knock!", "Creak!").
- Decision: **ID change** vocab[3].en "senior student" -> "older student", with seg5 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa/slide, conversation.lines[1].en, and seg6 say/subtitleEn/slide (A+B). seg5 definition -> "A big kid in your club." (it would be circular otherwise). seg18 "Kiko" -> "Haru", JA ハルくん (objective, story consistency).

#### L320 Club Posters
