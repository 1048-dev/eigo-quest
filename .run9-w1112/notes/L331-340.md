### Run 9 review notes: L331-340 (world 12, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles and spoken quiz choices, US praise, "Ding-dong!", "Give a thumbs-up.", lowercase after "Now, / Then, / Next,"). There are 68 patches, and all 10 lessons have changes. No learning item IDs change (no vocab, expression, TPR, phonics or target-expression text was touched). The one changed conversation line (L331 lines[0]) is not a target expression, and its slide copy changes with it. The main fix is again the "formula read aloud" pattern in the goal, "Here's the point" and answer segments ("Ask Are you, and Is he.", "Is he, so Yes, he is.", "I want to plus an action", "I want to join, plus I like, or I can."). I replaced these with model questions and answers or "..." fill-in pauses. The uncontracted "I am / He is / They are" in the grammar-target lines is kept on purpose (world 12 reviews the be-verb). The check script passes: ALL OK. The lessons are 207-231 s long with 89-91% English.

#### L331 I Am a New Student.
- A: "Today we visit a junior high school entrance ceremony." is not how a teacher states a plan ("Today, let's visit..."). In seg1, "I am, for me. You are, for you." and in seg8, "About yourself, I am. To your friend, You are." are formulas read aloud. "Birimo runs in, in a tiny uniform" is clumsy.
- B: lines[0] is 9 words (over the Stage-1 limit). The am/are rule is right, but the seg1 and seg8 fragments do not give a model sentence. In seg9, "Where is a different word." can be understood as a question. Sight words are/was/were plus the where contrast are fine.
- C: "in, in" is a stumble. "Where is a different word." is voiced with question intonation. The comma chains in seg1 and seg8 cannot be read naturally.
- Decision: lines[0], seg0 say/subtitleEn/slide.items[1] -> "Today, let's visit a junior high entrance ceremony." (A+B). seg1 -> "Say, I am a new student. Then say, You are in Class 1." plus the subtitleJa (A+B+C). seg7 -> "Then Birimo runs in. His uniform is tiny!" (A+C). seg8 -> "About you, say I am. To a friend, say you are. Are you nervous? Yes, I am." (A+B+C). seg9 -> "The word where is different." (A+C).

#### L332 He Is My Friend.
- A: "chatting in the yard" sounds like a home yard (JA こうてい). "A boy, he is. A girl, she is." (seg1) and "so he is / so she is" (seg8, seg19) are fragments. "You're a great introducer!" is not a real-sounding compliment.
- B: In seg6, "And who is she? She is Riko." has two speaker changes with no tag, so it sounds like Kiko asks and answers. The JA subtitle has the same gap. The th tip and "said rhymes with red" are correct.
- C: The speaker changes in seg6 cannot be voiced. "introducer" is awkward to say.
- Decision: seg0 yard -> schoolyard (A+B). seg1 "For a boy, say he is..." (A+C). seg6 add "I ask," / "Kiko says," and わたし / キコちゃん (B+C). seg8 and seg19 "so we say he is / she is / they are" (A+C). seg20 -> "You're great at introducing friends!" (A+C).

#### L333 Are You Ready?
- A: The conversation is natural ("Here it is! You should check your bag.", "He is tall, right?"). In seg1 "Ask Are you, and Is he.", seg8 "Are you comes back as I am." and seg19 "Is he, so Yes, he is." are formulas read aloud.
- B: The answer-with-the-same-words rule is the key point, but "comes back as" is hard to understand. Question-answer pairs teach it directly and still match the JA. The silent-l tip for should/could/would is correct.
- C: The three formula lines are unreadable. "Ding-dong! That's the school bell." is fine (a chime).
- Decision: seg1 -> "Ask, Are you ready? Ask, Is he your brother? Answer yes or no." (A+C). seg8 -> "Are you ready? Yes, I am. Is he your brother? Yes, he is. If not, say No, I'm not." (A+B+C). seg19 -> "Is he your brother? Yes, he is." (A+C).

#### L334 My Friend Poster
- A: The poster scene and "People call me the bread king!" are natural. "I like music, but she likes music." sounds like a contradiction.
- B: The third-person S rule is correct, and "With can, no S." is a good contrast. In seg9, "it says eh" is not a clear way to show the short e. lines[1] is 9 words with because ("because" is a phonics word here). I kept it because only one reviewer flagged it.
- C: TTS reads "eh" as the interjection "ay". "rhymes with end" is easy to say.
- Decision: seg8 -> "I like music. She likes music. For he or she, add an S." (A+B+C). seg9 -> "Friend. I, E, but it rhymes with end." (A+C).

#### L335 Hello, Junior High!
- A: The speeches are natural. aiTask "Please introduce yourself!" is cold coming from a child. In seg12, "At the end, Take a bow." has a capital after a comma. In seg19, "Two people, so they are." is a fragment.
- B: In seg8, "I am. I like. He is or she is." reads as finished sentences. "..." pauses like seg17 are clearer. In Kiko's "They are kind.", the referent is loose (JA みんな), but it is the quiz-answer slide for They are, so it stays.
- C: "Lights on!" and "Tap, tap. Is it on?" are fun. The seg8 fragments sound choppy.
- Decision: aiTask -> "Can you introduce yourself?" (A+B). seg8 "I am... I like... He is... or she is..." (A+C). seg12 lowercase "take" (objective). seg19 "so we say they are" (A+C).

#### L336 Do You Like Basketball?
- A: The club scenes are fun ("He kicks the basketball. Oops!", "Squeak, squeak go the shoes!"). In seg1 "Ask Do you like, and answer." and in seg8 "Do you like? ... Then add, I like, to share your favorite." are unfinished formulas. In seg20, "tell what you like" sounds off.
- B: The do/do answer rule is correct. The clap syllables bas-ket-ball and so on are correct. "Hit the shuttle." is OK, though US kids say "birdie" (TPR item, single flag, kept).
- C: "Do you like?" ends in the air. The rest reads well.
- Decision: seg1 -> "Ask, Do you like basketball? Then answer." (A+C). seg8 -> "Do you like basketball? ... Then share your favorite. I like soccer." (A+B+C). seg20 tell -> say (A+B).

#### L337 I Can Use a Computer.
- A: Natural ("Haru scratches his head.", "Which one smells better? Ha ha!"). "Ask Can you?" (seg1) and "Can you? Answer with can." (seg8) are unfinished questions.
- B: The "it's OK to say I can't" message is good. The stress tip ba-NA-na is correct. aiTask "I can cook pancakes!" ("make" is more common) was kept, because cook is the lesson verb.
- C: "Can you?" alone sounds cut off. "ba NA na" may be voiced unevenly by TTS, but it is the only way to show stress in text, so I kept it.
- Decision: seg1 and seg8 "Can you cook?" (A+C).

#### L338 I Want a New Racket.
- A: Natural and funny ("Science is hungry work!", "Game, set, match!"). In seg8, "I want plus a thing... I want to plus an action." is a formula.
- B: The want vs want to rule is correct, and the JA matches. The four-syllable words are fine. "Today we visit..." in seg0 is a little off, but only one reviewer flagged it.
- C: "I want to plus an action" is heard as the verb "plus".
- Decision: seg8 -> "For a thing, say I want. ... For an action, say I want to." (A+C).

#### L339 I Like It and I Can Do It!
- A: The art club talk is natural ("His picture is a big rainbow blob.", "It's fantastic! I like your flowers, Kiko.").
- B: The like/can/want-to chain is clear. The syllable counts (pen-cil 2, el-e-va-tor 4) are correct. EN and JA match.
- C: In seg1, "I like, I can, I want to." ends on "to" and sounds cut off.
- Decision: seg1 -> "I like... I can... I want to..." (A+C). No other change.

#### L340 Club Trial Review
- A: The club choices are natural ("I can eat a lot!"). In seg1 "Answer Do you like and Can you.", seg8 "I want to join, plus I like, or I can." and seg19 "Do you, so No, I don't." are formulas. "Great choosing!" is not natural praise. "A big paper on the wall" is a grammar slip.
- B: The do-vs-can answer quiz is a good review point. "Two friends can choose different clubs. That's OK!" is a good message. In seg17, "Then add I like, or I can." needs fill-in pauses.
- C: The formula lines and "or I can." endings sound cut off.
- Decision: seg1 -> "Answer, Do you like tennis? And, Can you cook?" (A+C). seg2 -> "A big sheet of paper on the wall." (objective). seg8 -> "I want to join the basketball club. I like sports." (A+C). seg17 "I like... or I can..." (A+C). seg19 -> "Do you like tennis? No, I don't." (A+C). seg20 -> "Great choices!" (A+C).

#### For the coordinator
- **Formula comma chains** are in almost every goal, point and answer segment of world 12 ("Ask Are you, and Is he.", "Is he, so Yes, he is.", "Do you, so No, I don't.", "so he is / so they are", "X plus a thing"). Expect the same template in L341-360.
- **Capital after a comma** in other lead-ins (not only Now, / Then, / Next,): "At the end, Take a bow." (L335). Grep for ", [A-Z][a-z]+ " TPR inserts after "At the end," / "First," / "After that," in other blocks.
- **"Today we visit ..."** (L331, L338): a simple-present plan. It is fine in short lines, but it is unnatural and too long in L331 (fixed). Others may exist.
- **"Please + verb" in aiTask starters** ("Please introduce yourself!", L335) is not caught by the lint (the line starts with "Hello!").
- ID changes: none.
- Kept on purpose: uncontracted "I am / He is / They are" in target lines (world 12 grammar review); "Ding-dong! That's the school bell." (L333, a chime); "Hit the shuttle." (L336 TPR item; "birdie" is more US, but it is an item); "I can cook pancakes!" (L337, lesson verb); "ba NA na" stress spelling (L337, TTS risk noted); Kiko's "They are kind."
