### Run 9 review notes: L431-440 (world 15, Stage 2)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, OK, thumbs-up, US praise, Ding-dong, Last time, "speed challenge", lowercase after "Now, / Then, / Next, / First,", L431 football->classroom). There are 52 patches in 10 lessons (every lesson has at least one change). There are no learning-item ID changes and no title changes. The most common fixes are: "tell ~" used for "talk about / give / say" (L431 "Tell every day from now", L438 "tell a reason", L439 "Tell the differences", L440 "tell your idea"); the made-up term "two-word words" (L432, L433, L435 -> "two-part words"); "Congratulations, everyone!" as an opener with nothing to congratulate (L435, L440); TTS-unfriendly fill-in models with a bare "ing" (L438, L439 seg17); and the block "First, in the picture" lowercase that now reads as "inside the picture" (L436, L440 -> "First, say In the picture"). Objective fixes: L435 calls a rainbow a "plains animal", L437 has a capital "In" mid-sentence, L431 JA チクッ for a clock tick, and three speed-challenge slides titled "Let's move!". Every new English sentence has 12 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. The touched lessons are 222-228 s long with 97-99% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L431 Every Day or Now?
- A: The soccer scene and the habit / now contrast are natural ("Do you play on weekends, too?", "Pass it to me!"). In seg1, "Tell every day from now." uses "tell A from B" (= distinguish), which is adult and abstract for a goal line. "Like, I read every day." in seg17 is casual but fine.
- B: Same flag on seg1: the child cannot tell what to do. The every day / usually vs now / Look! cue words are clear, and the quiz contrast is right. In the seg4 JA, チクッ is a needle-prick sound, not a clock tick (カチッ).
- C: Reads well ("Fast feet, fast feet!", "Goal! Woo-hoo!"). "This very second, tick!" is fine aloud.
- Decision: seg1 say/subtitleEn "Tell every day from now." -> "Every day or right now? Pick the right sentence." (A+B). seg4 subtitleJa チクッ -> カチッ (objective, JA).

#### L432 Pass the Ball!
- A: The game calls are real ("I'm open! Over here!", "Good pass!", "Good game, everyone! Let's shake hands."). "Today is a mini soccer game." in the script makes the day equal a game. "two-word words" (seg9) is not something a teacher says.
- B: The kind-words theme is well placed, and the compound words are concrete. "Pass the ball means kick it to me" is simple enough. The JA いくよ for "Here you go" fits the moment.
- C: "two-word words" stumbles (word-word). "Today is a mini soccer game" has a flat, odd stress. The rest reads well ("So many balls!").
- Decision: teacherScript "Today is a mini soccer game." -> "Today we have a mini soccer game." (A+C). seg9 say/subtitleEn "two-word words" -> "two-part words" (A+C).

#### L433 Keep Going!
- A: The marathon cheers are natural ("You're almost there! I can see the finish line!"). In seg8, "Keep going in the middle of the race." reads as an order to the child, not as "say Keep going". "help a friend never give up" (seg20) is awkward. "A very, very long race" is the stacked "very" from the style guide.
- B: Same flag on seg8: the when-to-say-what idea gets lost. The JA ほこりに おもうわ (line 5) is translationese; じまん is what an adult says to a child. "Cheering words give people power" is a little translated, but clear (single flag, kept).
- C: "two-word words" again (seg9). seg8 has odd stress with the phrases at the front. "A very, very long race" is OK aloud (single flag on its own, but A+B agree from the style guide).
- Decision: seg2 say/subtitleEn -> "A really, really long race." (A+B). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "In the middle of the race, say Keep going. Near the end, say You're almost there. After the race, say You did it!" (A+C). seg9 say/subtitleEn -> "More two-part words." (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> "..., so your friend won't give up." (A+C). conversation.lines[5].ja みんな ほこりに おもうわ -> わたしたちの じまんよ (objective, JA).

#### L434 Who's Winning?
- A: The tug-of-war is lively ("Heave, ho!", "Ha ha, everyone is on the grass!"). "Then answer short." (seg1) is not standard English. Birimo says "Now the blue team is winning! Who's winning now?", which answers his own question, but it rehearses the target (single flag, kept).
- B: Same flag on "answer short". The short-answer point ("You don't need to say winning again") is clear, and the is / does quiz contrast is good. The seg19 JA チャンピオン for "winner" is close enough (kept).
- C: Reads well. "Who is winning? The red team is." has a good rhythm.
- Decision: seg1 say/subtitleEn "Then answer short." -> "Keep the answer short." (A+B).

#### L435 Sports Day on the Plains
- A: The reporter frame is fun ("Back to you, Ms. Emma!", "Whoosh! I'm flying like a butterfly!"). The script opens with "Congratulations, everyone!" with nothing to congratulate. "to catch ears" (seg8) is a translation of みみを つかむ, not English. seg9 calls rainbow a "plains animal".
- B: Same flags on "Congratulations" and "catch ears". rainbow is not an animal (also in the JA いきもの and in tipJa), which is a factual error. The is / are contrast is clear.
- C: In seg7, Kiko's lines have no speaker cue, and "Then, what a race!" blends narration into her line. "catch ears" is also hard to make sense of aloud.
- Decision: teacherScript "Congratulations, everyone!" -> "Hello, everyone!" (A+B). seg7 say/subtitleEn -> "Kiko goes on. ... Kiko ends, What a race! ..." (A+C; the JA already marks Kiko). seg8 say/subtitleEn "to catch ears" -> "to get everyone's attention" (A+B+C). seg9 say/subtitleEn -> "Let's review two-part words from the plains.", subtitleJa そうげんの いきもので -> そうげんで みつけた, and tipJa 草原の いきものの 複合語 -> 草原で みつけた 複合語 (objective).

#### L436 In the Picture
- A: The studio talk is natural ("Look, that cloud is me!", "It looks just like you!"). "Please look at this picture." (line 0) is the stiff please-command from a teacher. In the script, Ms. Emma says "Ms. Emma is pretending to be the painter." about herself. In seg8, "First, in the picture." (lowercase from the block fix) reads as "inside the picture".
- B: Same flag on "Please look". The apostrophe explanation is clear. The JA かれ / かのじょ in expressions and lines is textbook style, but the product uses it in many worlds, so it is kept (reported below). The quiz distractor "He'm" is intentional.
- C: In seg9, the JA part starts with a bare "'", which TTS cannot read, so the spoken sentence has no subject. The third-person self-reference in the script also sounds odd. Same flag on seg8.
- Decision: conversation.lines[0].en -> "Take a look at this picture." (A+B). teacherScript "Ms. Emma is pretending" -> "I'm pretending" (A+C). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "First, say In the picture. Next, say who is doing what." (A+C). seg9 say ' は -> アポストロフィは (B+C; the subtitleJa keeps the mark for display).

#### L437 On the Left, On the Right
- A: The festival picture and the Birimo joke work ("Don't worry, Birimo. You're next!"). In the script, "And In the middle" has a capital mid-sentence. "If your friend is lost, help them." means lost in a place, which is not the picture context.
- B: Same flag on "is lost". The L-with-your-left-hand trick is correct and helpful. "On the right, they're eating lunch." has no antecedent for they, but it is the learning item and it is clear with the picture (kept).
- C: Reads well ("Left and right, all right!", "Boing!").
- Decision: teacherScript "And In the middle" -> "And in the middle" (objective), and "If your friend is lost" -> "If your friend can't find something" (A+B).

#### L438 What's Happening?
- A: The newspaper photo chat is natural ("Extra, extra!", "Let's help them tomorrow!" / "That's a good idea, Birimo!"). "You can tell a reason, too." in the script is unidiomatic ("give a reason"). "Great reporting today!" (seg20) is a loose fit for reading a newspaper (single flag, kept).
- B: Same flag on "tell a reason". The seg17 frame "Some people are... ing." has no real model sentence. The seg13 speed-challenge slide is titled "Let's move!", which does not match the other lessons.
- C: TTS will read a bare "ing" in seg17. "The i in is is missing" in the script stumbles on "is is"; seg9 already says "goes away".
- Decision: teacherScript "tell a reason" -> "give a reason" (A+B), "The i in is is missing" -> "The i in is goes away" (B+C). seg17 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa -> "Then answer with ing, like, Some people are running." (B+C). seg13 slide.title -> "Speed challenge" (objective).

#### L439 Spot the Difference
- A: The game is natural ("Found one!", "Found two! Three more differences. Keep going!"). "Tell the differences." (seg1) and "You can tell differences" (seg20) use tell for talk about, the same template the L381-390 review fixed.
- B: Same flag on "tell". The isn't / aren't contrast and the quiz are correct ("They isn't" is an intentional distractor). The seg17 model "the... is... ing" gives no full sentence. The seg13 slide title is "Let's move!".
- C: TTS will read "the... is... ing" and "isn't... ing" as broken pieces. "n-apostrophe-t" in the script reads OK as letters (single flag, kept).
- Decision: seg1 and seg20 say/subtitleEn "tell (the) differences" -> "talk about (the) differences" (A+B). seg17 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa -> "Like, In my picture, the dog is running. In your picture, it isn't running." (B+C). seg13 slide.title -> "Speed challenge" (objective).
