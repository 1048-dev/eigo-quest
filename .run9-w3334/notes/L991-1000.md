### Run 9 review notes: L991-1000 (world 34, Stage 4)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide), plus testPoints. I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (US praise, I say / I ask, Hi / Hello openings, "Let's pretend I'm" starters). There are 40 patches. Every lesson has at least one change, and no lesson was left as is. There are no changes to school.expressions[].en, vocab[].en, tpr[].en or phonics words, so no test or src line is affected. The grammar order is clean: "do you know where" first shows up in L995 and is not used before it, and no new text adds an order-pinned form (would you mind, could you tell me, recommend, I'm afraid, instead...). Themes: (1) **Ms. Emma in the third person in the teacherScript**: the block-wide fix covered say and subtitles, but all 10 teacherScripts in this group still had her own narration as "Ms. Emma says / asks / answers / sees / smiles / supervises us / her pouch" while she also says "we" and "us". I changed these to I / my / me, following the W15-16 precedent ("Ms. Emma is pretending" -> "I'm pretending"). Skye's note (L995) and card (L1000) still name Ms. Emma; that is right because Skye wrote them. (2) **Story and fact errors**: L994 Birimo's water bottle goes through security, so it has to be empty. In L996 the captain says "Good afternoon" right after a 10:45 takeoff, and he says drinks will come "in a few minutes" after Kiko already has her water. L997 says a visa means "Yes, we saw you". In L998, "by the sea, so we need one more plane" gives no real reason. In L1000 the "best part of the airport" model answer is the window seat. (3) **Number-reading wording**: "two numbers at a time" (L991, L1000) and "Three numbers?" (L994) mean digits. This is the same wording fixed in L906. (4) **TTS traps**: "Time time!" (L992), "goes up on A, and down on B" (L993; TTS reads the article a), "Or means a person" (L997), and "Ding, dong" (L994; the block keeper prefers Ding-dong). (5) **US usage**: "look out of the window" -> "look out the window" (L996, L1000), and "have a lovely stay" -> "have a great stay" (L999). I checked the numbers and they agree across lessons. Check-in is 7:15-10:00, boarding 10:15, the gate closes 15 min before departure, and Flight 205 leaves at 10:45 from gate 23B. Seats are 14A/B/C with Birimo in 15A and Ms. Emma in 15B, on a 3-3 plane where A and F are windows. Flight 1120 leaves at 4:20 from gate 41C and lands at 6:05. Passport control and customs come at the first airport, and the domestic connection comes after them. Yawning or chewing for the ears is right. Every new sentence has 13 words or fewer. The check script passes with ALL OK. All lessons are 216-227 s long with 100% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L991 The Airport of Wings
- A: The wing-shaped roof, Skye the albatross guide and the Wing Stamp Book note ("This is not a passport.") are warm and clear. seg12 "Now open your Wing Stamp Book, with two hands." sounds off; people say "with both hands", and L992 uses "Both hands". In the teacherScript Ms. Emma says "Ms. Emma smiles and holds up her travel pouch ... she says", though she narrates in first person.
- B: Flight-number reading (two oh five, eleven twenty; 0 = oh) is correct. seg9 says "We read flight numbers two numbers at a time", but those are digits, and 205 is read as two / oh five. The teacherScript itself says "in small parts". "Every corner means everywhere, near and far" is a good gloss.
- C: The numbers are written as words in say, which is good for TTS. The comma in "Book, with two hands" adds an odd pause. The teacherScript "Ms. Emma ... she says" sounds like two narrators.
- Decision: teacherScript -> "I smile and hold up my travel pouch. Your passports are safe in here, I say." (A+C). seg9 -> "We read flight numbers in two parts." (A+B; JA 2けたずつ kept). seg12 -> "Now open your Wing Stamp Book with both hands." (A+C).
