#### L121 Good Morning, World!
- A: Birimo's "Great! You are the best!" is stiff. People say "You're the best!" seg1 "Say hello at the right time." is a little loose because Good night is not hello, but kids understand it. seg17 "What time is it now?" asks for the time when the answer is a greeting. The JA makes it clear.
- B: The three greetings by time of day come with a clear sun/moon frame. EN and JA match. The phonics tie-in "best" -> Birimo's catchphrase is nice.
- C: "You are the best!" sounds stilted when read aloud, and the contraction flows better. The rest reads well ("Wow, you're so fast!" reuses the phonics word).
- Decision: lines[5] and seg10 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa "You are the best!" -> "You're the best!" (A+C). seg1 and seg17 are kept.

#### L122 Hello Around the World

- B: seg7 say (JA) says Riko and Haru both "asked" how they were, but Haru answers with I'm good. That is a wrong description. The vocab drill lines "Hello. Hello!" and "Friend. Friend!" follow the block pattern.
- C: No issues. "Give me a high five!" reads well.
- Decision: seg7 say JA -> "リコちゃんは … How are you? と きいて、ハルくんは I'm good. と こたえたね。" (objective+B).

#### L123 What's for Breakfast?
- A: Ms. Emma's breakfast line is a 10-word list. "Thank you for the food!" (for いただきます) is a calque, but it is a known way to explain the custom. TPR "Put your hands together." usually means "clap" to a US child.
- B: lines[1] is over the Stage 1 limit (lint warn). In the seg18 quiz, "Rice, please. Or I like rice." gives the choices as statements, while every other quiz in the block uses "X? Or Y?".
- C: The long breakfast list runs on. The quiz is read with falling intonation, so it doesn't sound like a choice.
- Decision: lines[1] + seg6 say/subtitleEn/slide item -> "Look! Toast from England and rice from Japan. Tortillas from Mexico, too!" (objective+B+C). seg18 say/subtitleEn -> "Rice, please? Or I like rice?" (objective+C). "Thank you for the food!" and the tpr are kept.

#### L124 See You Later!
- A: seg0 sets the scene at sunset ("The sun is going down. ゆうがた"), and then Riko says "Have a nice day!". At sunset an American would say "Have a good evening/night." "Say goodbye in a nice way." is OK.
- B: The lesson contradicts itself. seg8 says Have a nice day! is ぴったり for あさや ひる partings, but the scene is evening. In seg0, subtitleJa "みんな、やあ！" has an unnatural order ("やあ、みんな！" is the norm). seg17 subtitleEn shows "See you later. Have a nice day!", but say has only "Your turn." plus Japanese (template pattern).
- C: "It's a sunny afternoon." reads as well as the old line. No other issues.
- Decision: seg0 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa -> "It's a sunny afternoon." / いい てんきの ごご, and subtitleJa "やあ、みんな！" (A+B, objective JA). seg17 is kept (template).
