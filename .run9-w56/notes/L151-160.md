### Run 9 review notes: L151-160 (world 6, Stage 1)

The review covered school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). The Japanese was also checked for naturalness and EN/JA match. Items handled centrally were not proposed again: double end marks, the "A / B" quiz subtitles, the "Or b?" spoken choices, the "Brilliant!" replacement and L158 "How many cherries?". There are 32 patches in 8 lessons (L153-L160). L151 and L152 have no changes. No vocab, expression, tpr or phonics item IDs changed. L156's title changed from "Café" to "Cafe", and its seg0 slide item was changed to match. All patched lessons pass the check script: length 190-212 s and English share 60-66%.

Two patterns repeat in this group:
- The wrap lines "You can say Look at the sign." / "You can order with I want and please." mention phrases without quotation marks. All five were quoted (L153, L157-L160). This follows the W03-04 fix `You asked "How many balls?"`.
- The i_e lessons open the sound segment with "i, e with blends!" / "i, e review!". These were aligned with the a_e lessons' "Magic e with blends!" / "Magic e review!" (L158, L160). L156's "New magic e! i, e." introduces the vowel, so it is kept.

#### L151 Small Letters a to m
- A: "It's a small b." / "Capital B, small b." work, but a US teacher would more often say "lowercase b". This is the item wording for the whole unit (vocab "small letter"), so it stays. "You've got it!" is fine.
- B: There is one new pattern and the a-m range is clear. The b/d "tummy" hint (b right, d left) is correct. EN and JA match.
- C: The "c, a, k, e. Cake!" spelling rhythm is good. The aiTask starter says "this letter" twice, but it reads fine.
- Decision: no change.

#### L152 Small Letters n to z
- A: "The tail is on the right." is correct for q. "Jump like a small z." is a little odd, but it is a playful TPR item.
- B: Comparing similar letters (p/q, n/h, j/l) is a good, focused step. The j answer is review, so it is fine. EN and JA match.
- C: No issues. "Cap. Add an e. Cape!" is clear.
- Decision: no change.

#### L153 Look at the Sign!
- A: The signs vocabulary and "Wait, Birimo! The bus is here." are natural. The wrap "You can say Look at the sign." has a capital "Look" mid-sentence with no quotation marks.
- B: The green running-man exit sign is the Japanese style, but it fits the Japan setting. The quoted phrase in the wrap subtitle is unclear without quotation marks.
- C: Without quotation marks, the wrap is hard to phrase as a quote.
- Decision: seg20 say and subtitleEn -> `You can say "Look at the sign."` (objective+C).

#### L154 Letters Around Town
- A: "police box" is the Japanese English term for 交番, not a US word, but the setting is Japan and it is a vocab item. "Here is a town map." could be "Here's", which is taste.
- B: The conversation ja "こうばんだ。KOBAN！" uses capitals, but the line is about finding a small k and the English has "Koban!". tpr[1].ja uses the kanji 大きな, while the lesson's own subtitle writes おおきな. In seg8, "I see は みえるよ" reads like "I can see it!" rather than a gloss.
- C: In seg8, "I see は みえるよ" is ambiguous when read aloud. The other lessons use the "X は Y。" gloss (want は ほしい).
- Decision: lines[1].ja and seg6 subtitleJa "KOBAN！" -> "Koban！" (objective+B). tpr[1].ja 大きな -> おおきな (objective+B, style guide section 10). seg8 say -> "I see は みえる。" (B+C).

#### L156 Lunch at the Cafe
- A: The title and seg0 write "café", while scene and aiTask write "cafe". Every other world writes "cafe". The quiz "Spaghetti? Or sandwich?" needs "a". "Grrr..." for a tummy rumble is a little odd, but cute.
- B: Same flag on "Or sandwich?" (the lesson models "I want a hamburger"). In seg8, subtitleEn "Me too! OK." leaves a dangling "OK." that does not carry the Japanese "Me too! で オッケー".
- C: "Or a sandwich?" reads more smoothly. The dangling "OK." is odd if the subtitle is read.
- Decision: title, seg0 slide item, seg0 say and subtitleEn "café" -> "cafe" (objective). seg8 subtitleEn drops "OK." (A+B). seg18 say and subtitleEn -> "Or a sandwich?" (objective+B). "Grrr..." is kept.

#### L157 I Want a Parfait!
- A: "pudding" for プリン and "jelly" for ゼリー are the British and Japanese-English sense. A US child hears "jelly" as a jam-like spread, and "pudding" as a soft custard, not flan. Both are vocab IDs, and "jelly" = ゼリー is also used in world 1 (already reviewed), so they are kept and flagged for the coordinator. The seg8 subtitle has the same dangling "OK." as L156.
- B: The a / no-a explanation (a parfait / pudding) is good. Same flag on "OK.". seg8 say has a spacing typo "パフェ は".
- C: The wrap "You can say I want a parfait." reads OK. A quoted form keeps the group consistent.
- Decision: seg8 subtitleEn drops "OK." (A+B). seg8 say "パフェ は" -> "パフェは" (objective). seg20 say and subtitleEn quote the phrase (objective, consistency).

#### L158 How Many Cherries?
- A: The chef role-play is natural. In seg9, "i, e with blends!" is not something a teacher says. In the wrap, "You can say How many cherries?" has a capital "How" mid-sentence.
- B: The plural -s focus is clear. "Cherry. Two little red balls." describes the pair on one stem, and the JA matches.
- C: "i, e with blends!" is choppy to say. L153 uses "Magic e with blends!".
- Decision: seg9 say and subtitleEn -> "Magic e with blends!" (A+C). seg20 say and subtitleEn -> `You can say "How many cherries?" Three cherries, please.` (objective+C, kept at 8 words or fewer).

#### L159 My Dream Parfait
- A: "Do you remember I like? Like has magic e, too!" reads as a broken sentence without quotation marks. "cookies and cream" is a nice real flavor name.
- B: Joining things with and is one clear step. "and は と" in say is brief, but it matches the subtitle "and は「～と」".
- C: "Do you remember I like?" is confusing to read aloud. The quotation marks show the mention.
- Decision: seg9 say and subtitleEn -> `Do you remember "I like"? "Like" has magic e, too!` (objective+A+C). seg20 say and subtitleEn quote the phrase (objective).

#### L160 The Parfait Shop
- A: The shop role-play is natural. "What do you want?" from a shopkeeper is a bit blunt (US: "What would you like?"), but it is the unit's target. "You can order with I want and please." needs quotation marks. "i, e review!" has the same problem as L158.
- B: tpr[3].ja / action use 大きな / 大きく, while the lesson's subtitles use おおきな. In seg19, subtitleJa "こたえは Two, please. Here you are. は ..." runs two English phrases together.
- C: "order with I want and please" is hard to phrase aloud. "i, e review!" is choppy, and L155 uses "Magic e review!".
- Decision: tpr[3].ja and action -> hiragana (objective+B). seg9 say and subtitleEn -> "Magic e review!" (A+C). seg20 say and subtitleEn -> `You can order with "I want" and "please."` (objective+A+C).
