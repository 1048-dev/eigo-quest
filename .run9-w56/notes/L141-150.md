### Run 9 review notes: L141-150 (world 5, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not propose the block-wide mechanical fixes again (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A? Or b?" quiz says, US praise), and I did not undo them. There are 42 patches in 8 lessons (L141, L142, L143, L144, L147, L148, L149, L150). L145 and L146 have no changes. Two learning item IDs change, both TPR items: L147 "Take a rest." -> "Take a break." and L149 "Pack your pencil case." -> "Put your pencil case in your bag.". Every field that repeats them (say, subtitleEn, slide items and the L147 conversation line) was updated. No vocab, expression or phonics IDs changed. Every sentence stays at 8 words or fewer. The check script passes: all touched lessons are 200-221 s long with 54-64% English.

#### L141 Do You Have a Pen?
- A: The borrowing dialogue is natural ("Here you are!", "What a cute rabbit!"). "Riko says, I have an eraser." uses the unquoted goal/wrap style found in every lesson. "Cut long words in two!" is not quite how a US teacher talks about syllables (they say "break it into parts"), but it is understandable.
- B: The level and progression are right, and the do/do answer rule is clear. In seg9, subtitleEn says "Long words are easy. Just cut them in two!", but the say has only "Cut long words in two!", so the subtitle shows text that is never spoken.
- C: The script reads well. "Rub, rub, rub!" and "Write, write, write!" have a good rhythm.
- Decision: seg9 subtitleEn now matches the say (objective). Everything else is kept.

#### L142 I Have a Ruler.
- A: Riko's "Let's use my scissors together." is a direct translation of いっしょに つかおう. One pair of scissors is not used together; a child would say "Let's share my scissors." The wrap "You can say, I have, and I don't have." is a comma chain.
- B: "share" is the useful word for this exchange, since Haru has no scissors. EN and JA still match, because いっしょに つかおう means sharing here. The phonics tip is correct: each word has two short vowels.
- C: "You can say, I have, and I don't have." is hard to phrase aloud, because each comma forces a pause.
- Decision: conversation line 3, seg7 say/subtitleEn/slide -> "Let's share my scissors." (A+B). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can say "I have" and "I don't have."` (A+C, the same quoting as the L73 wrap fix).

#### L143 My Pencil Case
- A: The shaped pencil cases (a cactus, a beetle) and "So cute!" work well. In the wrap, "You showed your pencil case in English!" is a calque of しょうかい. Children don't "show something in English"; they talk about it.
- B: The same flag on the wrap: the Japanese しょうかい できたね means "talked about / introduced", not "showed". "Great thinking!" is fine.
- C: "Break it into two." is fine to read. "I have, と しょうかい してね" has an odd comma, but TTS handles it.
- Decision: seg20 say/subtitleEn -> "You talked about your pencil case in English!" (A+B, 8 words).

#### L144 How Many Pencils?
- A: The counting talk is natural ("How many crayons, Riko?", "I have one pencil... in my pocket!"). "You've got it!" is fine US praise.
- B: The plural rule is taught in two different ways. Pencils get "2ほん いじょう", but crayons get "たくさん / いろが いっぱい あるから s". That suggests the s comes from "many colors". testPoints[0] "かずと ふたつ いじょうの s で いえる" is broken Japanese. The quiz slide shows "I have three pencils." while the choices are about five pencils, but slide items must be lesson items, so it stays.
- C: Easy to read. "ズボンの ここ、ぽんぽん" is cute.
- Decision: vocab[1].ja, seg3 say and seg3 subtitleJa now use the same 2ほん いじょう rule (objective+B). testPoints[0] Japanese fixed (objective+B).

#### L145 Stationery Swap
- A: The lending dialogue is natural. "Stationery" is a British/formal word (US kids say "school supplies"), and "swap" means exchange, not lend. "Say thank you and bow." is a Japanese custom, not something a US teacher would say.
- B: The title mismatch (swap vs かしかり) is real. But the title is also listed in content/curriculum/titles.json, which is outside these patches, so I left it to the coordinator. The rest of the review lesson (color + supply, Here you are / Sorry) is clear, and EN and JA match.
- C: The script reads well. "Click!" for a magnet repeats the stapler sound from L143, but that is fine.
- Decision: no change. The title is flagged for the coordinator (A+B, but titles.json is outside this scope).

#### L146 My Morning
- A: "Did you sleep well? Are you still sleepy?", "Wake up, Birimo!" and "What a nice morning video!" are all natural.
- B: Greeting, time, weather and breakfast are linked clearly. The vocab word "sunshine" appears only in its vocab segment (info only). The blend tip is correct.
- C: In the phonics drill "St, a, mp. / Cr, u, st.", TTS will read "St" as "Saint/Street" and "a"/"u" as letter names. This is a block-wide pattern (see the final report), not specific to this lesson.
- Decision: no change.

#### L147 My Afternoon
- A: TPR "Take a rest." and Birimo's "Let's take a rest" sound non-native or British. A US teacher says "Take a break." "after snack" in the aiTask starter is normal US classroom talk.
- B: "Take a break" is the far more frequent chunk, so it is more useful to learn. The TPR item, the speed challenge and the conversation all repeat it, so they must change together. The JA ひとやすみ fits both. The words "drift" and "flask" are a bit hard, but the Japanese gloss for flask helps.
- C: "Pr, i, nt" / "Fl, a, sk" have the same TTS letter-name issue (block-wide). "Take a break" is as easy to say as "take a rest".
- Decision: tpr[3] "Take a rest." -> "Take a break." (A+B, **item ID change**), plus seg13/seg14 say, subtitleEn and slide, conversation line 4 and seg8 say/subtitleEn/slide.

#### L148 My Week
- A: Kiko's presentation is natural ("Dance class!", "I like every day!"). The aiTask starter "What day do you like?" is textbook English. A child would ask "Which day do you like?" or "What's your favorite day?".
- B: "What day do you like?" is easy to confuse with "What day is it?", which this lesson also teaches. seg9 say "ようびの あとには s" states a false rule, because "It's Thursday." in the same lesson has no s. The plural s belongs to "I like Saturdays" / "on Saturdays" (every Saturday).
- C: The script reads well. "Stamp, stamps. Gift, gifts." has a clear rhythm.
- Decision: aiTask.starterEn -> "Which day do you like?" (A+B). seg9 say -> 「どようびが すき」は おわりに s を つけて、I like Saturdays. (objective+B).

#### L149 Ready for School!
- A: TPR "Pack your pencil case." sounds like "fill your pencil case (with pencils)", but the Japanese and the action both mean putting it in the bag. "school bag" is OK (backpack is more common, but it is an item and is glossed as ランドセル).
- B: Same flag: an EN/JA meaning mismatch on a TPR item. "Put your pencil case in your bag." is clear, recycles L143's "Put it in your bag." and has 7 words.
- C: "Mi, lk / He, lp": TTS will read "He" as /hiː/, the same block-wide issue. The new TPR line reads fine in the speed challenge.
- Decision: tpr[0] -> "Put your pencil case in your bag." (A+B, **item ID change**), plus seg11/seg13 say, subtitleEn and slide.

#### L150 World 5 Grand Review
- A: "Let's write our week!" is not natural English; it should be "Let's write about our week!". "Ding, dong!" is normally written "Ding-dong!" (as in L65/L80).
- B: Same flag on "write our week". The seg20 quiz answer "What time is it!" repeats the right choice, which is a question, with "!" (the block rule fixed this in L73/L76/L79). teacherScript "あしたは ボスせんだよ" assumes the next session is tomorrow (compare the L72 きのう fix).
- C: With "What time is it!", the line would be read as an exclamation. "Ding, dong!" gives TTS an odd pause.
- Decision: conversation line 3 + seg8 say/subtitleEn/slide -> "Let's write about our week!" (A+B). "Ding, dong!" -> "Ding-dong!" in line 4 and seg8 (objective). seg20 say/subtitleEn "!" -> "?" (objective+C). teacherScript あしたは -> つぎは (objective+B).
