#### L171 First, Then
- A: The sandwich scene and Birimo's joke work. seg0 "Today, we make sandwiches." uses the simple present for something happening now, and a US teacher says "Today, we're making sandwiches." In seg19, "Sorry, Birimo! You're a super chef!" sounds as if Birimo is the super chef, but he is the one who got it wrong. The aiTask starter "What do you do then?" means "at that time" to a native ear.
- B: First/Then is presented one at a time with gestures, which is good. The aiTask starter should cue the target "Then, ~.", and "What do you do then?" does not model it. "Close the sandwich." is a bit odd, but it is a TPR ID and kids understand it.
- C: "we make sandwiches" is flat to read, and "we're making" flows better. "Sorry, Birimo! You're a super chef!" makes the actor aim the praise at Birimo. "What do you do then?" ends on a weak word.
- Decision: seg0 say/subtitleEn -> "Today, we're making sandwiches." (A+C). seg19 say/subtitleEn -> "Sorry, Birimo! You're all super chefs!" and subtitleJa -> "みんな スーパー シェフだね！" (A+C). aiTask starterEn -> "First, I wash my hands. Then...?" (A+B+C, same open prompt as L174).

#### L172 The Hungry Rabbit

#### L173 Let's Make Pancakes!
- A: The cooking-show lines are natural. "Mix it round and round" is fine in US English too. "Then, cook it" after "the eggs" uses "it" for the batter, which is normal in recipe talk.
- B: phonics.sounds gives the short o in hop/not as /ɒ/, which is British. US is /ɑ/, and world 2 already uses /ɑ/. Line 2 JA "フライパンで やく。ひっくりかえす！" is a plain statement while the EN is an instruction ("cook it", "Flip!"). The other recipe lines use the te-form (まぜて, かけて). seg18 subtitleJa has "Mix the eggs？ Cook it？" with full-width ？ after English, which the block's "A / B" form fixes. "it" after "eggs" may confuse some learners (B only).
- C: Reads well. "Flip, flip, flip!" is fun.
- Decision: phonics.sounds[0] "/ɒ/" -> "/ɑ/" (objective, US). Line 2 JA and seg7 subtitleJa -> "やいて。ひっくりかえして！" (objective+B). seg18 subtitleJa -> "Mix the eggs? / Cook it?" (objective). "Then, cook it." is kept because it is an item ID and only B flagged it.

#### L174 Story Time
- A: Ms. Emma says "The end. Do you like the story?" right after finishing the story, but a native teacher says "Did you like the story?" The aiTask starter "Once upon a time, I'm a little cloud." puts a past-narrative opener and present tense in one sentence, which no native storyteller does. seg17 ("Once upon a time, I'm a...") has the same problem.
- B: The opener/closer pair (Once upon a time / The end) is clear, and First/Then is recycled well. The starter models the tense clash for the child to copy. Splitting it as "Once upon a time... I'm a little cloud!" keeps Stage 1 present tense and the role-play, and it matches conversation line 0 "Once upon a time... Look! A little cloud." "Did you like" is a fixed classroom chunk, so it is fine at this level.
- C: The comma version gives no breath after the opener. With "..." TTS pauses naturally, which is what a story reader does. "Do you like the story?" after "The end." sounds wrong when read aloud.
- Decision: conversation line 4 and seg8 say/subtitleEn/slide -> "Did you like the story?", with line 4 JA and seg8 subtitleJa -> "おはなし、きにいった？" (A+C). aiTask starterEn -> "Once upon a time... I'm a little cloud!" (A+B+C). seg17 say/subtitleEn -> "Once upon a time... I'm a..." and subtitleJa "むかし むかし… わたしは ～。" (A+B+C).

#### L175 Our Picture Book

- B: This is the item the child repeats and uses on their own (seg14, seg17), so the clash should not be modeled. The fix is clear and needed, and it is the same shape as L174. The JA むかし むかし、わたしは ちいさな ねこ。 can stay.
- C: Same as L174: "..." gives the opener its pause. The quote punctuation does not change the reading.
- Decision: **item ID change** expressions[1] "Once upon a time, I'm a little cat." -> "Once upon a time... I'm a little cat." (A+B+C). It was updated in conversation line 0, seg6 say/subtitleEn/slide, seg8/seg14/seg20 slides, seg14 say/subtitleEn and seg17 say/subtitleEn. "'The Little Cat'." -> "'The Little Cat.'" in line 0, seg6 and seg19 (objective, US punctuation). The tpr "Read with a big voice." is kept (A only; item ID).

#### L177 My School Day
- A: The school-day talk is natural. "Let's play chase!" is a real kids' game name, and chase is the phonics word. "lunch time" is usually one word ("lunchtime") in US dictionaries, but the two-word form appears more than 20 times in world 5 and world 6, so it should be decided centrally (see Kept).
- B: seg9/seg10 split the words as "Sh, a, pe" / "Wh, i, te" / "Th, o, se" / "Ch, a, se". That chunks the silent e into a voiced syllable, which contradicts the lesson's point (おわりの e は しずか) and the subtitleJa/tip "sh-a-p-e". In seg17 the say "じぶんの する ことで いってね" and the subtitleJa "へやの なまえで OK" give different instructions.
- C: TTS will read "pe", "te" and "se" as odd syllables ("pee", "tee"). Spelling them out as "Sh, a, p, e." matches "S, t, o, n, e." used elsewhere in world 6 and reads cleanly.
- Decision: seg9 and seg10 say/subtitleEn -> "Sh, a, p, e. Shape! Wh, i, t, e. White!" / "Th, o, s, e. Those! Ch, a, s, e. Chase!" (B+C). seg17 say -> "へやの なまえで いいよ。" to match subtitleJa (objective+B).
