### Run 9 review notes: L381-390 (world 13, Stage 2)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A... or b..." says, OK, thumbs-up, US praise, "Last time" in L382, lowercase after "Now,", TPR capitals mid-sentence) or the central decisions (fisher, Well done!, Japanese in `say` ending with ".", letter spell-outs like "d g e"). There are 40 patches in 10 lessons (every lesson has at least one change). There are no learning-item ID changes and no title changes. The most common fix is "tell about ~" / "tell a friend's day" / "tell your favorite things" (unidiomatic for "talk about") in L382, L387 and L389. Others: made-up question chains in L381 ("Same like? / New news?"), TTS non-words in L384 ("Badg", "Thehedgehogeatsporridge"), teacher jargon in L387/L390 ("frequency words", "speed TPR"), and broken model fragments ("She always has. Haru never goes."). Every new English sentence has 12 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. The touched lessons are 210-228 s long with 97-98% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L381 Really? Me Too!
- A: The lighthouse chat is natural ("Cool! I live near the big bridge.", "Can you see the sea from your house?"). In seg8, "Same like? Say Me too! Same don't like? ... New news?" is not English a teacher would say. "He says he is a local kid" could contract, but it is fine.
- B: The dge after a short vowel tip is correct, and the three reactions are introduced one at a time. Same flag on seg8: "Same don't like?" models a wrong structure right where the lesson teaches "Me neither". EN and JA match elsewhere.
- C: The seg8 chain cannot be read naturally ("New news" trips). The comma in seg3 "with your arms, over the water" is a little odd, but readable (single flag). TPR capitals mid-sentence are the block pattern.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "You like it, too? Say Me too! You don't like it, either? Say Me neither. Something new? Say Oh, I see." (A+B+C). The subtitleJa already says this and is kept.

#### L382 How About You?
- A: The plaza ball game and "Conversation is like playing catch" work well. "Hug your heart." is not a gesture phrase in English. "You can tell your favorite things." (seg19) is unidiomatic, and it should be "talk about".
- B: "Hug your heart" gives an unclear action (JA むねを ぎゅっと). Same flag on seg19 "tell your favorite things". The dge words after short u/o are well chosen. "Last time" (block fix) is consistent.
- C: Reads well ("Bounce, bounce.", "Wow, nobody got hit!"). The comma splice "You throw a question, your friend throws it back." is fine aloud.
- Decision: seg3 "Hug your heart." -> "Hands on your heart.", JA むねに てを あてて (A+B). seg19 "tell your favorite things" -> "talk about your favorite things" (A+B).

#### L383 What Kind of Music?
- A: The harbor concert is natural ("Rock star answer!", "DJ Emma's speed mix!"). "Kiko's tail is swinging" is a little odd but OK. "Let's enjoy the concert" is fine because enjoy has an object.
- B: "What kind?" as a follow-up question is a good next step after L382. "The steady pulse of a song" is above level (single flag, kept, because defining beat with beat would be circular). In seg1, the JA それとも ふたことで uses a question word in a statement.
- C: Reads well. "Gad, get, clap!" is clear syllable rhythm.
- Decision: seg1 subtitleJa それとも ふたことで What kind? -> ふたことだけで What kind? でも いいよ。 (objective, JA).

#### L384 Sorry? One More Time, Please.
- A: The windy pier fits the asking-again skill. Birimo's 15-word line "The hedgehog eats porridge from the fridge, and the badger eats fudge on the bridge!" is a deliberate fast tongue twister that causes "Can you speak more slowly, please?". It is natural for that purpose. "Can you speak more slowly, please, Birimo?" stacks please and the name, but it is acceptable.
- B: The learner never has to produce the tongue twister, so its length is intentional (kept). "Badg, er." splits on a non-word. "Badge, er." links to badge from L381. The aiTask starter is a 14-word run-on over the Stage 2 limit. "Thehedgehogeatsporridge!" shows a non-word in the subtitle.
- C: TTS will mangle "Badg" and the run-together "Thehedgehogeatsporridge". Hyphens keep the fast, joined feeling and stay readable. The starter reads better as two sentences.
- Decision: seg10 "Badg, er." -> "Badge, er." in say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa (B+C). seg17 -> "The-hedgehog-eats-porridge!" (B+C). The JA katakana is kept. aiTask.starterEn split into "The wind is so strong today! And I talk super fast when I'm excited." (B+C). conversation.lines[4] is kept (intentional).

#### L385 Chat Time at the Harbor
- A: The lantern-night chat with all four tricks is natural ("Just kidding, I don't eat bugs!"). "Lizard. Small, green, and a long tail." mixes adjectives and a noun.
- B: The dge / ge contrast (short vowel vs long vowel or consonant) is correct. "Pet the puppy" uses pet as a verb in the pet definition, which is a mild double meaning (single flag, kept). EN and JA match.
- C: The seg5 list stumbles at "and a long tail". The "Really. What kind. Sorry. How about you." count in seg8 reads OK as a list.
- Decision: seg5 -> "Small and green, with a long tail." (A+C).

#### L386 Harbor Quiz Time
- A: The guess-who quiz is natural ("I'm thinking of a person in this town. Ask me questions!", "Is it the fisher? Yes! You got it!"). "I start." is not how a speaker takes the first turn. Say "I'll go first."
- B: The does/work point ("The s jumps to does!") is clear, and the quiz contrast "Does she works? / Does she work?" fits. The JA わたしから fits "I'll go first".
- C: "I start." is abrupt and flat at the head of a long segment. The rest reads well ("Bzzzt!", "Ding ding ding!").
- Decision: seg6 "I start." -> "I'll go first." (A+C).

#### L387 My Friend's Day
- A: The ship setting and early bird / night owl are natural and fun. "Tell about a friend's day" (seg1) and "You can tell a friend's day with s" (seg18) are unidiomatic. "frequency words" in the script is teacher jargon. "Use and, then, and after that to connect" sounds like a garbled list.
- B: Same flag on "tell about". "She always has. Haru never goes." (seg8) are broken fragments that model a missing object. "frequency words" is unknown to a child. The -ies / goes / has review is correct.
- C: The seg8 fragments sound cut off. The script list "and, then, and after that" cannot be phrased clearly.
- Decision: seg1 and seg18 "tell (about)" -> "talk about" (A+B). seg8 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa -> "She always has a notebook. Haru never goes to bed early." (A+C). teacherScript "use frequency words" -> "use words like always and never" (A+B), and the tip -> "Connect your sentences with and, then, or after that." (A+C).

#### L388 A Postcard from the Harbor
- A: The post office scene and Birimo's "B, R, I, J?" are natural. In seg8, "Then your nickname." conflicts with the script ("and your name"). People sign postcards with their name. "How do you spell the bridge word?" is awkward.
- B: Same flag on nickname vs testPoints (さいごの なまえ). "Your card goes in here, and off it goes!" repeats goes and uses an idiom inside a long clause. The tch/dge spelling review is correct.
- C: The seg4 double "goes" is hard to phrase. "the bridge word" is clunky aloud. The stray comma "Start with Dear, and a name" makes a wrong pause.
- Decision: seg4 -> "Put your card in here. Off it goes!" (B+C). seg8 -> "Start with Dear and a name. ... Then your name.", JA じぶんの なまえ (A+B). seg17 -> "How do you spell bridge?" (A+C). The JA はしの bridge is kept.

#### L389 Sunrise Harbor Review
- A: The goodbye walk is warm ("Take off your shoes. The sand is warm!", "We'll miss you, Sunrise Harbor!"). "Tell about the harbor people" (seg1, seg19) is unidiomatic. "And the seagull still watches my sandwich!" would be "is still watching" for right now, but the habitual reading (it still does it) is grammatical.
- B: Same flag on "tell about". Keeping "watches" keeps the tch + -es review word, so the seagull line is kept (single flag). The doesn't + base verb quiz is correct.
- C: Reads well ("Say cheese!", "Step, step.").
- Decision: seg1 and seg19 "tell about" -> "talk about" (A+B).

#### L390 World 13 Grand Review
- A: The speech show is natural, and the cliffhanger to the market works. "First, speed TPR!" and "A frequency sentence." in the script are teacher jargon. In seg1, "What your friend loves, always, their day, and ..." uses "always" alone as a list item, which is not a phrase. "Kiko waves her tail" (wags is more usual) is a single flag (kept).
- B: Same flag on TPR / frequency jargon. The four-part speech frame is clear otherwise. The recap JA スピード TPR is also jargon (single flag, kept, reported).
- C: The seg1 list is 16 words and stumbles on "always". Short parts read naturally. The seg8 "Loves. Always. Their day. Doesn't." is a mnemonic echo of the slide and reads fine.
- Decision: teacherScript "speed TPR" -> "speed moves", "A frequency sentence." -> "Something she always does." (A+B). seg1 say/subtitleEn -> "What your friend loves. What they always do. Their day. Something they don't do." (A+C).
