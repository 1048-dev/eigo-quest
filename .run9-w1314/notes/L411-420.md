### Run 9 review notes: L411-420 (world 14, Stage 2)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide), plus testPoints. I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes or the central decisions (fisher, "sweets", "Well done!", "Cheers!", "Tomorrow is the lunch box contest", Japanese inside `say` ending with ".", letter spell-outs like "T Y"). There are 36 patches in 9 lessons. L416 has no changes. There are no learning-item ID changes and no title changes. The most common fixes are: formula read aloud as a word list or comma chain in "Here's the point" / "Today's goal" (L413, L414, L415, L417, L420 x2), and "Ty" / "ty" used for the /ti/ ending, which TTS reads as the name Ty (/taɪ/) (L411, L413 x2, L419). Every new English sentence has 12 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. The touched lessons are 207-232 s long with 98% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L411 Foods Around the World
- A: The world food corner talk is natural ("People in Japan! And Kiko!"). "People means lots of persons" in seg18 is stiff; a teacher says "more than one person", like the teacherScript. "Sneaky!" for forty is fun.
- B: The People + eat (no s) point and the -ty tip are clear and correct. "lots of persons" also models a rare plural. EN and JA match.
- C: "Ty, ty!" in seg10 will be read as the name Ty (/taɪ/), not the /ti/ ending. The rest reads well.
- Decision: seg10 "Ty, ty!" -> "Tee, tee!" (A+C). seg18 -> "People means more than one person, so eat has no S." (A+B).

#### L412 Breakfast Here and There
- A: The traveler role play is natural. The seg20 wrap-up "compare breakfasts with in Japan, many people" and "ask what do you usually eat" blend a description with quotes and are not real teacher talk.
- B: "Many, not all!" and the eighty/ninety spelling tips are good. In seg11 the JA おさらへ (plate) does not match the EN "into the bowl".
- C: The seg20 line cannot be phrased naturally (no place for the quote intonation). The rest reads well.
- Decision: seg20 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa -> "You can compare breakfasts. In Japan, many people eat rice. You can ask, What do you usually eat?" (A+B+C). seg11 subtitleJa おさらへ -> ボウルへ (objective, EN/JA mismatch).

#### L413 Where Does It Come From?
- A: The warehouse scene is natural. In the teacherScript, "For one thing, we say comes." sounds like the idiom "for one thing" (= first of all). seg8 "It comes from." and "Bananas come from." stop in the middle.
- B: The come/comes contrast is the right point, but "One cheese?" models a count error in a world that teaches countable/uncountable nouns. The -teen/-ty tip is correct.
- C: The seg8 fragments drop in pitch with nothing after "from". "Ty" (teacherScript, seg10) is read as /taɪ/.
- Decision: teacherScript "For one thing" -> "For one food", "Ty is short" -> "T Y is short" (A+C). seg8 -> "This cheese? Where does it come from? It comes from a farm. Many bananas? Bananas come from warm places.", JA follows (A+C). seg10 "Ty, quick!" -> "Tee, quick!" (A+C).

#### L414 Healthy Lunch Box
- A: The contest prep talk is natural ("I want fried chicken and cake!", "One piece of cake, please! Just one!"). seg8 "Vegetables, many things, so are. Rice is one thing, so gives with S." is a telegraphic formula, not teacher talk.
- B: Same flag on seg8: the are/gives contrast is the key point and should be said in full sentences. "sweets" kept (central decision). hundred/thousand tips are correct.
- C: seg8 is choppy and hard to phrase. "Th, thousand!" may be read as letters (single flag, kept).
- Decision: seg8 -> "Vegetables means more than one, so we say are. Rice is one thing, so we say gives, with an S." (A+B+C).

#### L415 Lunch Box Contest
- A: The contest is natural and warm ("The winner is... all of you!"). seg8 "My lunch box has, then the colors, then It's colorful and healthy." is a formula comma chain. "rice, eggs and broccoli" without the serial comma is fine (taught as "and before the last one").
- B: The three-step presentation model is clear. "Your real lunch box or a dream lunch box, both are OK." is a comma splice but fine in speech (single flag).
- C: seg8 cannot be read naturally. The rest reads well ("Clap, clap, clap!").
- Decision: seg8 -> "Three steps. First, say what's inside. Next, say the colors. Then say, It's colorful and healthy.", subtitleJa まず なかみ (A+C).
