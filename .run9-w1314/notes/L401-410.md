### Run 9 review notes: L401-410 (world 14, Stage 2)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A... or b..." says, OK, "Give a thumbs-up.", US praise, lowercase after "Now,"), and I left the central decisions alone ("Two thumbs up!", "Thumbs up, sound masters!", Japanese inside `say` ending in "."). There are 45 patches in 7 lessons (L402, L403, L404, L406, L408, L409, L410). L401, L405 and L407 have no changes. There are no learning-item ID changes. The most common fixes are: speaker changes in the "Let's listen" retellings with no "I say," / "Birimo says," (L403, L408, L410); quotes that run into the sentence ("for safety, Be careful...", "say Be careful! to keep..."); comma chains of the three answers in L409 ("A little, yes, very, or no, not at all"); and the mixed taste/texture line "It tastes sweet and soft." Every new English sentence has 12 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. The touched lessons are 211-226 s long with 97-98% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L401 What's in the Soup?
- A: The soup guessing game is natural ("Can you guess?", "Fish soup next time, please!", "Knock, knock. Who's there? Soup!"). "Whoo, whoo." for blowing on soup is a little odd, but it is a fun sound.
- B: Countable "there are" vs uncountable "there is some" is clear and the quiz contrasts them well. "There are lots of carrots, so we say there are" gives a plural reason, not the countable reason from seg8, but it is not wrong (single flag). EN and JA match.
- C: Reads well. The quiet-K "Shh, it's sleeping!" / "Don't wake up the K!" is easy and fun. "Whoo" may sound like a cheer (single flag).
- Decision: no change.

#### L402 Cut the Carrots
- A: In the conversation, Ms. Emma says "First, wash your hands." Then Riko asks "What's next?" and the answer is "First, cut the carrots." That is two "First"s in a row. "Today, we start cooking ourselves!" can be heard as "cooking ourselves". "Tell the cooking steps" is a little stiff (single flag).
- B: The two "First" steps confuse the order lesson. Hand-washing is the prep, so "Before we start," fixes it without touching the expression "First, cut the carrots." In seg8, "for safety, Be careful" runs a quote into the sentence. In seg20, "say Be careful! to keep friends safe" puts an exclamation mark in the middle of a sentence.
- C: "cooking ourselves" makes listeners smile for the wrong reason. The seg8 and seg20 quotes cannot be phrased naturally.
- Decision: conversation.lines[0] en/ja, seg6 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa/slide -> "Before we start, wash your hands." (A+B). seg0 -> "Today, it's our turn to cook!" (A+C). seg8 -> "And for safety, say, Be careful with the knife." (A+C). seg20 -> "You can say, Be careful! It keeps your friends safe." (A+C).

#### L403 Mix It Well
- A: The pancake class is natural ("Salt? In pancakes?", "My arm is tired!", "Don't stop! Keep mixing."). "A recipe starts with action words" is a little loose (each step does), but fine.
- B: Then as a linking word is clear, and the silent-W quiz is good. In seg6, "Yes. Mix it well." comes after Kiko's question with no speaker, so it sounds like Kiko. The JA has the same gap.
- C: Without "I say," the voice cannot show the speaker change in seg6. "Choo choo!" is fun.
- Decision: seg6 say/subtitleEn -> "... right? I say, Yes. Mix it well. ...", JA わたし「そう。…」 (B+C).

#### L404 It Smells Good!
- A: "It tastes sweet and soft" mixes taste and texture. "Very, very good to eat!" is on the style guide's avoid list. "a feeling word, like good or sweet" is wrong: US teachers call these "describing words".
- B: The JA for "feeling word" is ようすの ことば (describing word), so EN and JA do not match. In teacherScript, "have an s because it is one thing" is a confusing explanation of third-person -s. The JA says it simply (it の ときは s). The looks/tastes quiz is excellent.
- C: Reads well. "Ding! The timer rings." is fine.
- Decision: conversation.lines[4] and seg7 say/subtitleEn/slide -> "No, it doesn't. It's sweet and soft." (A+B). seg8 "feeling word" -> "describing word" (A+B). seg5 -> "Really, really good to eat!" (A+B). teacherScript -> "After it, we add an s. Smells, tastes, looks." (A+B).

#### L406 Try This!
- A: The sample corner talk is natural ("Look! Free samples!", "Oops, I have crumbs on my face."). In seg20, "Great tasting today!" sounds like the adjective "great-tasting". In seg5, "Another way to say very good to eat" is clumsy.
- B: Offering and accepting are well paced. The mb tip is correct. For seg5, "delicious" (learned in L404) is the clean gloss, and the JA 「とても おいしい」 is its gloss.
- C: In seg5, "say very good to eat" is hard to phrase. "Great tasting" gets the wrong stress.
- Decision: seg5 -> "Another way to say delicious." (A+C). seg20 -> "Great job at the sample corner today!" (A+C).

#### L408 I Can't Eat Eggs
- A: The allergy scene is natural and well handled ("Let me check. Yes, it does. Sorry.").
- B: In seg9, "Touch your top teeth to your lip" is unclear. The JA says したくちびる, and L409 says "bottom lip". In seg7, Birimo's answer and Riko's reply have no speakers, so the whole line sounds like Riko. The JA has the same gap.
- C: The seg7 voice cannot show three speakers without tags.

#### L409 Spicy, Sour, Salty
- A: The world food street is natural ("Wow! Hot, hot! Water, please!", "It's sweet corn soup."). "Squeeze your face!" is not idiomatic. The US phrase is "Scrunch up your face!". In seg17 and seg20, "A little, yes, very, or no, not at all" blurs the three answers.
- B: The JA かおを くしゃっと matches "scrunch up". The teacherScript says the ph is "in the middle or at the end", but all four words have it in the middle (objective). tipJa says なかや おわりにも as a general rule, which is true (graph), so it stays.
- C: The comma chain of answers cannot be read clearly. One answer per sentence works (as in seg1).
- Decision: teacherScript "in the middle or at the end" -> "in the middle" (objective). seg3 -> "Scrunch up your face!" (A+B). seg17 -> "Then answer. A little. Yes, very. Or no, not at all." (A+C). seg20 -> "You can ask about tastes and answer in three ways. A little. Yes, very. No, not at all." (A+C).

#### L410 Food Stand Talk
- A: The food stand review is natural ("Lamb-shaped cookies!", "Here's some water."). "What a food stand!" sounds unfinished or even sarcastic. Mixing "guests" (vocab) and "customers" (TPR ID) is fine.
- B: The JA for seg20 is すてきな やたい, so "great" restores the meaning. In seg7, "Yes, please!" after Riko's question has no speaker. The JA has the same gap.
- C: In seg7, the voice needs "I say," to switch back. "Thumbs up, sound masters!" is a central keep.
- Decision: seg7 say/subtitleEn -> "I say, Yes, please!", JA わたし「…」 (B+C). seg20 -> "What a great food stand!" (A+B).

#### For the coordinator

- **Quotes run into a sentence** ("for safety, Be careful with the knife", "You can say Be careful! to keep friends safe"). Fixed in L402. Check the seg8/seg20 wrap-ups elsewhere.
- **Comma chains of answer options** ("A little, yes, very, or no, not at all") fixed in L409 seg17/seg20.
- "Touch your top teeth to your lip" (L408) -> "bottom lip". Other ph/f/v sound segments may have the same wording.
- Kept on purpose: "Whoo, whoo." (L401 blowing), "Then add a happy word, like It was delicious!" (L407), "The steps to make a food." (L405), "Tell the cooking steps in order." (L402), L409 tipJa なかや おわりにも (general rule), "guests" vs TPR "customers" (L410).
