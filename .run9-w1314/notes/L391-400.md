### Run 9 review notes: L391-400 (world 14, Stage 2)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A... or b..." says, OK, US praise, lowercase after "Now,", "Cheers!", "Well done!", Japanese in `say` ending in "."). There are 27 patches in 8 lessons (L399 and L400 have no changes). There are no learning-item ID changes and no title changes. The most common fixes are clipped "rule fragments" in "Here's the point" and answer lines ("A glass of. / A cup of.", "How much is. / Socks are two, so are.", "None? Any again.", "so don't have any"), and lines that model the very error the lesson teaches against ("a small, round bread" in L393, "two milks" in the L394 quiz). The lint hits in L393 ("breads" in teacherScript, "a bread" in seg18) are intentional: one is the "We don't say one bread, two breads" contrast, the other is the wrong quiz option. Both are kept. Every new English sentence has 12 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. The touched lessons are 219-225 s long with 97-98% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L391 Welcome to the Market!
- A: The market arrival is natural ("Wow, look at that!", "Excuse me, excuse me. It's crowded!"). In teacherScript, "For one thing, say there is." sounds like the idiom "for one thing" (= firstly). The seg8 shorthand "One fountain, there is. Many shops, there are." is terse but OK for a point slide.
- B: The is/are contrast and the -es /ɪz/ tip are clear and well paced. The expression JA どこにも りんごの はこが あります is a bit odd (どこにも usually goes with a negative; あちこちに is more natural), but it is a learning-item field and the meaning is clear (single flag, kept).
- C: Reads well. "Two foxes in glasses, riding buses!" is a fun line. "For one thing" gets the idiom's intonation.
- Decision: teacherScript "For one thing" -> "For just one thing" (A+C).

#### L392 Do You Have Any Peaches?
- A: The fruit-shop role-play is natural ("Fresh fruit here!", "How about some pears?"). In seg19, "No cherries, so don't have any." has no subject. "What a juicy answer!" is fine.
- B: The some/any rule is clear. The seg8 "None? Any again." is too clipped for learners. seg19 should model the full "we don't have any".
- C: "None? Any again." is choppy and hard to phrase. In seg7, Ms. Emma's line after "Oh no." has no "I say", but she is the seller, so it works (single flag).
- Decision: seg8 "Any again." -> "Use any, too." (A+C). seg19 -> "so we don't have any" (A+B).

#### L393 Some Bread, Please
- A: The bakery scene is natural ("it smells so good in here!", "You're the best!"). "Do you want some butter?" is a little plain for a shopkeeper, but it is fine for this level. seg3 "A small, round bread." uses "a bread", which this lesson calls wrong.
- B: The lint hits are intentional: "We don't say one bread, two breads." is a contrast, and "Can I have a bread?" is the wrong quiz option. But seg3 "a ... bread" contradicts the lesson point. teacherScript already says "Rolls are small pieces of bread".
- C: Reads well. "No a with bread." may sound like "no ah" in TTS, but it is short and clear in context (single flag).
- Decision: seg3 say/subtitleEn -> "A small, round piece of bread." (objective, contradicts the lesson point). The teacherScript "breads" and the seg18 "a bread" are kept on purpose.

#### L394 A Glass of Juice
- A: The juice stand is fun ("Oops! I drank it already."). seg8 "A glass of. / A cup of." is not real speech. The seg10 "Hello, hello, echoes!" sounds like greeting the echoes. "One juice, two juices? No." is a classroom simplification (Americans do order "two orange juices"), but it is fine for the teaching point.
- B: The quiz premise "You want two milks." models the uncountable error the lesson and L395's quiz teach against. The /z/ vs /ɪz/ contrast for -oes is clear.
- C: Lines ending on "of" are awkward to say. "Hello! Hello! Hear the echoes?" gives a better echo effect.
- Decision: seg8 -> "A glass of milk. ... A cup of tea." (A+C; subtitleJa keeps the formula). seg10 -> "Hello! Hello! Hear the echoes?", JA follows (A+B). seg18 "two milks" -> "milk for two", JA ふたりぶんの (A+B).

#### L395 Shopping List Game
- A: The team game is natural ("Tomatoes, check! Bread, check! We win!"). "Tick!" in seg11 is British for a check mark. "high five the air" uses the verb, which is spelled "high-five".
- B: The count/no-count review is clear (two apples vs some milk). Same flag on "Tick!". "Need means we must have it." is OK for this level.
- C: Reads well. "Done!" is easy to say.
- Decision: seg11 "Tick!" -> "Done!", JA ピッ -> よし (A+B). seg12 "high five the air" -> "high-five the air" (objective, spelling). The TPR interjection "High five!" is kept.

#### L396 What Does It Cost?
- A: The shop talk is natural ("Excuse me. How much is this hat?", "Hats off to you!"). seg8 "How much is. / Socks are two, so how much are." and seg19 "Socks are two, so are." are broken. The aiTask "What do you like?" is not what a clerk says.
- B: The is/are and y->ies tips are correct. Same flag on the aiTask starter: it reads as a general "what do you like?" question. "consonant" is a hard word, but the JA しいん supports it (kept).
- C: The seg8 and seg19 fragments cannot be read naturally. "Two socks, so we say are." flows.
- Decision: seg8 -> "One hat? How much is this hat? Two socks? How much are these socks?" (A+C). seg19 -> "Two socks, so we say are." (A+C). aiTask starterEn -> "What are you looking for?" (A+B). The title "What Does It Cost?" is fine (not changed).

#### L397 Fifty, Sixty, a Hundred
- A: The bookstore is natural ("Books, books, books, all the way up!"). teacherScript "When a word ends in f, the f changes to v" is too broad (roof, chef). The fifteen/fifty tip is helpful.
- B: Same flag: tipJa says every f word changes, but seg9 correctly says "Some words". The level of big numbers is fine.
- C: Reads well. "Top teeth on your lip for the V. Vvv." is good.
- Decision: tipJa -> ... ものが あるよ (A+B). teacherScript -> "In some words that end in f, the f changes to v. Then we add e-s." (A+B).

#### L398 That's Too Expensive!
