### Run 9 review notes: L481-490 (world 17, Stage 2)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "Yes, I am. Or no, I'm not." says, OK, US praise, "I say", lowercase after "Now, / Then, / Next, / First, / After that, / Finally,", L490 seg20 "That's it for today.", L483 "key chain" -> "keychain"). There are 24 patches in 9 lessons. L487 has no change. There are no learning-item ID changes and no title changes. The English in this world is already clean and natural, so the fixes are small. Themes: (1) TTS problems in syllable and sound spell-outs ("Ob, ser, va, tion", "I, ma, gi, na, tion", "cher" read like the name Cher); (2) wording a teacher would not use ("star goods", "a big paper", "That uneasy feeling about later", "A what question needs a plan", "feel safe" for a worried friend); (3) punctuation that breaks the reading (a question mark mid-sentence, a stray comma, capitals mid-sentence, a sentence starting with lowercase "t, i, o, n", a fragment with no verb); (4) one UK spelling ("night-time"). Every new English sentence has 12 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. The touched lessons are 210-227 s long with 97-98% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L481 The Observatory on the Hill
- A: The arrival scene is natural ("Look! There's a white building on the hill!", "We found it!"). seg1 "When you already made a plan, use going to." is normal American usage (simple past with already), so I kept it.
- B: going to for plans is introduced clearly, and the "No S, no E D" rule fits the quiz. In seg10, the syllable model "Ob, ser, va, tion" gives the wrong vowel (/vɑ/ instead of /veɪ/) and spells the ending tion instead of the sound shun that the lesson teaches. The JA ヴェイ・ション is already right.
- C: TTS will read "va" as "vah" and "tion" as "tee-on". The rest reads well.
- Decision: seg10 say/subtitleEn "Ob, ser, va, tion." -> "Ob, ser, vay, shun." (B+C).

#### L482 What Are You Going to Do?
- A: The cafeteria scene and the "game of catch" image for How about you? are natural. In seg18, "A what question needs a plan, not yes or no." is not how a teacher says it. The question doesn't need a plan. The answer is a plan.
- B: The yes/no distractor is a good check. The teacherScript says "Today, we have a day off. So let's talk about plans for the weekend.", which is a small logic jump (single flag, kept).
- C: In seg18, "It's A! A what question..." stumbles on the double "A".
- Decision: seg18 say/subtitleEn -> "It's A! For a what question, answer with your plan, not yes or no." (A+C). The JA already says よていで こたえるよ.

#### L483 I'm Going to Visit My Grandma

#### L484 Are You Going to Join Us?
- A: The invitation scene and the hot-cocoa twist are natural. In the teacherScript, "If you say no, tell your plan." needs a listener ("tell them your plan"). In seg4, "A big paper on the wall" uses paper as a countable noun. seg2 "Me too!" as a picture of "join" is a little odd but harmless (single flag, kept).
- B: The kind-refusal pattern (No, I'm not. + plan + Maybe next time!) is good. In seg12, "For Yes, I am, Nod your head. For No, I'm not, Shake your head." has capitals mid-sentence. In the teacherScript, "Long words, but the end is easy. t, i, o, n." starts a sentence with a lowercase letter.
- C: In seg10, the clap model "I, ma, gi, na, tion" will be read as "eye, ma, ghee, nah, tee-on".
- Decision: teacherScript "tell your plan" -> "tell them your plan" and "t, i, o, n." -> "It's t, i, o, n." (A+B). seg4 -> "A big sheet of paper on the wall." (objective). seg10 -> "Ih, ma, jih, nay, shun." to match JA イ・マ・ジ・ネイ・ション (B+C). seg12 "Nod / Shake" -> "nod / shake" (objective).

#### L486 Will It Rain Tomorrow?

- B: "I think it will. / I don't think so." is well chosen for a guess. The -sion /ʒən/ words are correct.

#### L488 It Won't Be Cold
- A: The worried-Kiko scene is warm. seg1 "Help a friend feel safe." and seg18 "You help friends feel safe." use "safe", which is about danger. For a worry, a teacher says "feel better", and Kiko says "I feel better now." herself. seg5 "That uneasy feeling about later." is not natural. "It's fifteen degrees." would sound freezing to an American (Fahrenheit), but Japanese kids think in Celsius, so it is a single flag and kept.
- B: Same flags on "feel safe" (JA あんしん is "relieved") and on seg5 ("uneasy" is above level). The teacherScript says "cher", but seg9 says "chur".
- C: In seg8, "just in case means, to be safe" has a stray comma that breaks the phrase. "cher" will likely be read like the name Cher (/ʃɛr/).
- Decision: seg1 and seg18 "feel safe" -> "feel better" (A+B). seg5 -> "Worry. You keep thinking about what might happen. Your tummy feels funny. Worry!" (A+B). seg8 comma removed (A+C). teacherScript "cher" -> "chur" (B+C). The JA is unchanged because it already matches.

#### Block-wide notes
- **Syllable spell-outs written as letters, not sounds** (L481 seg10, L484 seg10): TTS reads "tion" as "tee-on", "I" as "eye" and "va" as "vah". I fixed them here with sound spellings ("vay, shun", "Ih, ma, jih, nay, shun"). Scan the other groups for ", tion." and clap-the-beats lines.
- **"cher" vs "chur" for -ture**: the teacherScripts in L488 and L489 used "cher" (fixed), and the segments use "chur". Check L491+ teacherScripts for "cher".
- **"Zhun" for /ʒən/** (L486, L490): odd but consistent, so it is kept. If TTS misreads it, change all instances together.
- **"fifteen degrees"** (L488 conversation, seg6, seg11): this is Celsius, which an American would hear as freezing. It is kept because the learners are Japanese. If the owner wants strict US usage, change it to "It's sixty degrees." with JA 15ど kept, or add "Celsius". This is a learning-adjacent conversation line, so it is not changed here.
- **phonics.tipJa "ブロック1 の まとめ:" / "ブロック2 の まとめ:"** (L485, L490): an internal planning label with an ASCII colon, shown in a kid-facing tip. Consider "ふりかえり:" or dropping it. Not changed here.
- **Quoted-phrase capitals mid-sentence** ("say It will be sunny and It will rain", "That's all ends it"): this is the product-wide convention for quoted model sentences, so it is kept. A plain imperative after a comma ("For Yes, I am, Nod your head") is not a quote and was fixed (L484).
