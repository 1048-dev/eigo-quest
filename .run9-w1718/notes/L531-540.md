### Run 9 review notes: L531-540 (world 18, Stage 2)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "Yes, I am. Or no, I'm not." says, OK, US praise, "I say", lowercase after "Now, / Then, / Next, / First, / After that, / Finally,", "speed challenge"). There are 37 patches in 10 lessons. Every lesson has at least one change. L537 has only one small fix. There are no learning-item ID changes and no title changes. The comparison English in this world is clean and well sequenced, so most fixes are small. Themes: (1) wrap-up lines that join two skills with ", and" into 15-17 words, over the Stage 2 limit (L531, L536, L538, L540 seg0); (2) word-for-word glosses a native speaker would not say ("more far", "the bag of Riko", "in all time", "Tell the differences", "Put on your magnifying glasses"); (3) logic slips (the echo saying "Our echo is bigger than ever!", "Every choice is OK" after a two-way question, a bare "Because ..." line); (4) TTS problems ("like fl", "I say it, you are the echo", lowercase quoted model answers); (5) one JA line that dropped "louder". Every new English sentence has 12 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. All ten lessons are 208-218 s long with 97-98% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L531 Animal Superpowers
- A: The quiz show with the little frog is fun ("A frog with no flies is an unhappy frog!", "Faster than a cheetah!"). The teacherScript gloss "Farther means more far." teaches the wrong form ("more far") that the lesson is trying to replace. The facts check out (penguins swim faster than ducks, kangaroos can clear a car).
- B: Same flag on "more far" (L314 fixed "more tall" for the same reason). The un = opposite rule is clear. The seg19 wrap-up is 17 words.
- C: Reads well. "Boing, boing!" and "Ribbit, ribbit!" are easy. The seg19 sentence is too long for one breath.
- Decision: teacherScript "Farther means more far." -> "Farther means a longer way." (A+B). seg19 split into "... with can, er, and than. And you know un words." (A+C).

#### L532 Which Is Older?
- A: The old-town tour is natural ("Grab your hats.", "I'll fly up and check."). In the teacherScript, the model answers are written lowercase ("say, the tower is. Or, the tower is older."), unlike every other quoted line in the product. "Say just the winner" for the older thing is playful and fine.
- B: The short-answer rule (The tower is.) and the one-tower-so-is quiz are well chosen. "Un can also mean undo" is a clear second meaning of un-.
- C: "Or, the tower is older." reads aloud as Ms. Emma's own statement, not a model answer.
- Decision: teacherScript -> "To answer, say, The tower is. Or say, The tower is older." (A+C).

#### L533 Compare Two Pictures
- A: The spot-the-difference game is natural ("Maybe it was you, Birimo!", "Sharp eyes, detective!"). seg0 "Put on your magnifying glasses." is wrong (you hold one, and the JA says もって). seg0 "Ms. Emma has two pictures" has her talk about herself in the third person. seg1 "Tell the differences." sounds like the idiom "tell the difference" (= tell apart).
- B: Same flag on seg1 (JA ちがいを つたえよう = talk about them) and on seg0 (EN/JA mismatch). "untidy" is understood in the US, but "messy" is the everyday word (learning item, kept, reported).
- C: The third-person "Ms. Emma has" is odd in her own voice. The rest reads well.
- Decision: seg0 -> "Get out your magnifying glasses. I have two pictures of the valley." (A+B, A+C). The JA (エマ先生は) is natural Japanese teacher talk and stays. seg1 -> "Talk about the differences." (A+B).

#### L534 My Comparison Sentences
- A: The read-aloud circle and Birimo's cookie joke are fun. "Riko's bag means the bag of Riko." (teacherScript, seg8) is a word-for-word gloss of リコの かばん that no English speaker uses. Kiko's "Because you always choose the biggest one" has no why-question before it. A child would say "That's because ...".
- B: Same flags on "the bag of Riko" and the bare "Because" fragment, which is a known learner error and a bad model. seg2 "from a big letter to a dot" uses kid words, but this is a Stage 2 writing lesson, and the JA says ピリオド. "capital letter" and "period" are the school terms (as in L448). "Compare things, not people" is a good kindness note.
- C: "use 's" in seg19 may be read as a bare "s", but it is clear with the slide (single flag, kept).
- Decision: teacherScript and seg8 "the bag of Riko" -> "the bag that belongs to Riko" (A+B). seg2 -> "from a capital letter to a period" (A+B). conversation.lines[5], seg7 say/subtitleEn and seg7 slide.items[1] "Because you ..." -> "That's because you ..." (A+B). The JA ...えらぶからね already fits. No tests reference these lines.

#### L535 Echo Valley Quiz Show

#### L536 The Biggest Echo
- A: The planning meeting is warm ("Let's shout kind words, not just hello."). The teacherScript gloss "Ever means in all time." is not idiomatic. The fixed phrase is "of all time". seg14 "I say it, you are the echo." is a comma splice.
- B: Same flag on "in all time". The Let's (strong) vs Why don't we (softer) point is a good, simple contrast. The seg18 JA こだまが どんどん げんきに なってる drops "louder" (the conversation JA has おおきく、げんきに). The seg19 wrap-up is 16 words.
- C: seg14 sounds clipped aloud. seg19 is too long for one breath. "Hello? ... hello..." works well as an echo.
- Decision: teacherScript -> "Ever means of all time." (A+B). seg14 -> "I say it, and you're the echo." (A+C). seg18 subtitleJa -> どんどん おおきく、げんきに なってる (objective, JA). seg19 split into "... with Let's and Why don't we. And you made the biggest echo ever." (A+C).

#### L537 Thank You, Echo
- A: The thank-you scene is natural ("Goodbye, Echo! You're the best!", "Pinky promise!"). "We'll come back again." is a little redundant, but it is very common speech and a learning item (kept). seg9 "Ful sounds soft, like fl." is not a model a teacher gives.
- B: Same flag on seg9. The JA (ful は「フル」より かるく) means "lighter than フル", not "like fl". Thank you for + ing and the "for play" quiz are well chosen.
- C: TTS may spell out "fl" or read it as a strange syllable. "Very, very softly." for a whisper is natural teacher talk (kept).
- Decision: seg9 "Ful sounds soft, like fl." -> "Say ful short and soft." (A+B+C).

#### Block-wide notes
- **Wrap-up lines joining two skills with ", and you ..."** (L531, L536, L538) go over 12 words. I split them with "And you ...", the same fix as L421, L423 and L430. Check the last segment of every lesson for this template.
- **Word-for-word glosses in "X means ~" lines**: "more far" (L531), "the bag of Riko" (L534), "in all time" (L536). Other worlds may have the same "～ means the ～ of ～" possessive gloss or "more + short adjective".
- **Lowercase quoted model answers in teacherScripts** ("say, the tower is." in L532). Elsewhere the product capitalizes quoted sentences after "say,". A scan for `say, [a-z]` in teacherScripts may find more.
- **Ms. Emma in the third person** in her own segments ("Ms. Emma has", L533 seg0). The block fix covered only "Ms. Emma says". A scan for "Ms. Emma (has|is|wants|likes)" inside teacher.segments say may find more.
- **"untidy"** (L533 phonics and vocab context) is understood in the US, but "messy" is the everyday word. It is a phonics learning item (un- + tidy), so I kept it and am reporting it.
- **TTS stress spell-outs** (vol-CA-no, CAM-er-a, AN-i-mal) are kept as in earlier worlds. If the TTS reads the capitals as letter names, add a lexicon hint.
- Kept on purpose: "big letter" in Stage 1 worlds (only L534, a Stage 2 writing lesson, was changed to "capital letter"); L535 "Speed round!" in the quiz show; L537 "We'll come back again." and "Very, very softly."; L539 "the tallest" for a mountain and "I think so, too!" after the quiz; L540 seg18 long skills list; L534 seg19 "use 's"; L538 vocab visitor おとずれる ひと.
