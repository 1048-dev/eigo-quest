### Run 9 review notes: L511-520 (world 18, Stage 2)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "Yes, I am. Or no, I'm not." says, OK, US praise, "I say" narration, lowercase after "Now, / Then, / Next, / First,"). There are 39 patches in 10 lessons. Every lesson has at least one change. There are no learning-item ID changes and no title changes. The world 18 comparison lessons are clean and lively, so most fixes are small. Themes: (1) the wrap-up segment packs "You can X, and you can Y" into one 13-17-word sentence (8 lessons), split into two; (2) wrong or loose spelling and stress rules ("one vowel and one last letter? Double it!", "In English, the middle part is strong", "just add it"); (3) wording a teacher would not say ("Ask with which and better, A or B", "Both is OK", "about as heavy as twenty elephants, or more", "say the most popular"); (4) Japanese slips (どの 花が いちばん for "more beautiful", びっくりする 先生 for "surprising teacher", さむい ひと, うけるよ for "I can float"); (5) the deliberate wrong form spelled two ways (beautifuler / beautifuller). Every new English sentence has 12 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. The lessons are 210-225 s long with 97-98% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L511 Hello! Hello! Hello!
- A: The echo opening ("Hello... hello... hello!", "Echo, are we right? Right... right... right!") is fun and natural. seg8 "Put e r on a short word" sits next to "bigger" (doubled g), but the examples it gives (tall, loud) are right, so it is fine for a first lesson.
- B: One new pattern (～er than) with tall/long, which need no spelling change, is a good first step. The seg17 quiz slide shows "mountain / valley" rather than the choices (single flag). seg19 has a 17-word sentence.
- C: seg19 "You can compare two things with e r and than, and you can make a big echo." is too long to read in one breath.
- Decision: seg19 say/subtitleEn split into two sentences (A+C).

#### L512 Taller and Shorter
- A: The tree-measuring scene and Birimo flying to the treetops are natural. The script's "Stand up, please." sounds cold from a teacher to a child (style guide 4). TPR "Crouch down small." is a little odd ("crouch down low" is more usual), but it is a learning item and clear (single flag, kept).
- B: Same flag on "Stand up, please." The this/that near/far point and the e-ending spelling rule are clear. seg19 has a 13-word sentence.
- C: Reads well. seg19 runs long.
- Decision: teacherScript "Stand up, please." -> "Everyone, stand up!" (A+B). seg19 say/subtitleEn split (A+C).

#### L513 The Longest River
- A: The river walk and the "safest way" quiz are good and safety-minded. "This river is very, very long." is fine for storytelling (lint info only, kept).
- B: The doubling rule is stated wrong. seg9 says "A short word with one vowel and one last letter? Double it!", and the script says "one letter at the end". Every word has one last letter, and long or deep do not double. The tipJa already says 子音 1つ. The script sentence is also 15 words.
- C: seg9 reads fine aloud, but the rule is wrong.
- Decision: seg9 say/subtitleEn -> "One short vowel and one consonant at the end? Double it!", with subtitleJa さいごに 子音が 1つ (objective, wrong rule). teacherScript -> "Big has a short vowel and one consonant at the end. So we double the g." (objective).

#### L514 Heavier Than an Elephant?

- B: Same flag on seg18, and the JA だいたい おなじ … いじょう is muddled too. "heavier than twenty elephants" reuses the target pattern and is true (about 25 elephants). seg19 has a 13-word sentence.
- C: seg18 is hard to say with the right stress. seg3 "Whoo!" for blowing a feather may be read as "who" (single flag, kept).
- Decision: seg18 say/subtitleEn -> "It's heavier than twenty elephants!", JA ぞう 20とうより おもいんだよ！ (A+B+C). seg19 split (A+C).

#### L516 More Beautiful

- B: Kiko's "Which flower is more beautiful, Ms. Emma?" comes after Riko (blue) and Haru (yellow) disagree, so the comparative fits, but the JA どの 花が いちばん きれい is the superlative. seg7 subtitleJa repeats it. seg19 has a 16-word sentence.

- Decision: teacherScript "beautifuler" -> "beautifuller" (objective, consistency). conversation.lines[3].ja and seg7 subtitleJa -> どっちの 花が きれい (objective, JA). seg19 split (A+C).

#### L517 The Most Popular Animal
- A: The zoo survey and Ms. Emma's snake vote are funny and natural. Birimo's "Who chose the snake?" after "chose pandas / koalas" is a little uneven, but it is fine as "the snake option" (single flag, kept).
- B: seg9 "In English, the middle part is strong." is a wrong general rule, and the same line says pineapple is strong at the start. The JA えいごでは has the same problem. The JA いちばん びっくりする 先生 means "the teacher who gets surprised most". "Surprising" is びっくりさせる.
- C: Reads well. "ba-NA-na. to-MA-to." stress marks are clear.
- Decision: seg9 say/subtitleEn -> "In these words, the middle part is strong.", JA この ことばは (objective, wrong rule). conversation.lines[6].ja and seg7 subtitleJa びっくりする -> びっくりさせる (objective, JA).

#### L518 Better and Best

#### L519 Which Do You Like Better?
- A: The dinner chat and Birimo's "I like soup better. Can I have more?" are charming. seg8 "Ask with which and better, A or B." is not a sentence a teacher would say. "Both is OK, too!" is ungrammatical.
- B: Same flags on seg8. Giving the full model question is clearer for a child. seg3 subtitleJa さむい ひと reads as "a cold person". seg19 has a 17-word sentence.
- C: seg8 stumbles at "with which and better". seg19 runs long.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "Ask, Which do you like better, A or B? ... You can say both, too!" (A+B). The JA already matches. seg3 subtitleJa -> さむい 日と しろい ゆき (objective, JA). seg19 split (A+C).
