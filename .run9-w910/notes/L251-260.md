# Run 9 review notes: L251-260 (world 9, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, testPoints, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not repeat the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A? Or b?" quiz says, US praise, "やあ、みんな！", "Now, point"), and I did not undo any of them. There are 56 patches, and all 10 lessons change (L251-L260). Most patches are one pattern: the "Here's the point" lines and the wraps that list bare phrases in a comma chain ("You can ask Where do you want to go, and say I want to go to Italy.", "You can, then the thing to do, then in and the country."). I quoted them, following block rule 11 and the L142/L181 fix. No learning item IDs change (no vocab, expression, TPR or phonics strings), and no slide items change. Every new sentence has 8 words or fewer except the L255 wrap ("Then suggest with "Let's go to" and "You can."", 9 words, shorter than the old one). The check script passes (ALL OK): the lessons are 206-223 s long with 86-89% English.

## L251 Where Do You Want to Go?
- A: The world-map talk is natural ("Where should we go?", "It looks like a boot! Kick, kick!"). Riko repeats "Where do you want to go?" instead of "How about you?", but repeating the target is the point. seg8 "Answer with I want to go to, and a country." and the wrap "You can ask Where do you want to go, and say I want to go to Italy." are bare comma chains.
- B: The level is right. "too は 〜も" is a clear gloss, and the eat/go quiz is a good meaning check. The er tip (smaller and more muffled than ar) is correct. fern is explained (シダ).
- C: seg8 and seg20 force odd pauses at the commas. The rest reads well.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Answer with "I want to go to" and a country.` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can ask "Where do you want to go?" and say "I want to go to Italy."` (A+C).

## L252 I Want to See the Pyramids
- A: The cafe talk is natural. seg2 "Make round eyes with your fingers." means "look surprised" to a native speaker, not the finger-glasses gesture. seg19 "Pyramids are to see, not to eat!" is not how a teacher talks. seg1/seg8/seg20 have "Ask Why," and "Answer with I want to see, or I want to eat." with a stray capital and bare phrases. "They are very big." could be "They're", but the full form is fine (A only).
- B: Why + "I want to see / eat" is clear. The gesture for "see" is unclear in English. The ir tip and the sh warning for shirt are correct. conversation.lines[0] is 9 words ("Why do you want to go to Egypt, Haru?"), but it is the lesson target plus a name, so it stays. seg17 "I want to see... Or I want to eat..." keeps "Or" capitalized after "..." (left to the block rule).
- C: "Pyramids are to see, not to eat" is clunky. The comma chains in seg8 and seg20 break the rhythm.
- Decision: seg1 say/subtitleEn -> `Ask "Why?" and say what you want to do.` (A+C). seg2 say/subtitleEn -> "Make glasses with your fingers." and subtitleJa -> ゆびで めがねを つくって (A+B). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `"Why" asks for the reason. Answer with "I want to see" or "I want to eat."` (A+C). seg19 say/subtitleEn -> "We see pyramids. We don't eat them!" (A+C; the JA already says this). seg20 say/subtitleEn quoted the same way (A+C).

## L253 You Can Eat Pizza in Italy
- A: Poster making in the library is natural. seg8 "You can, then the thing to do, then in and the country." is the exact pattern named in block rule 11. In the wrap, "You can say You can eat pizza in Italy, and you can read ur words." is confusing because of the double "You can". "Make your own poster line." is a bit unusual ("poster sentence"), but it is understood (A only).
- B: "You can + verb + in + country" is broken down well. "er, ir, ur all the same" is correct. The "A purple nurse? Funny!" joke helps memory. おうひさま for queen is fine.
- C: seg8 is hard to say as one phrase. "Knock, knock! Hello!" and "Boing, boing!" read well.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Say "You can" and something to do. Then add "in" and a country.` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can say "You can eat pizza in Italy," and you can read ur words.` (A+C).

## L254 It's a Beautiful Country
- A: The quiz-show frame is fun and natural ("Kiko buzzes in."). seg12 "At the top, Take a picture." has a stray capital mid-sentence. seg8 "A country, then is famous for, then the special thing." is a comma chain. The wrap has the same problem.
- B: "famous for" with maple syrup is concrete. "Her, sir, fur. They rhyme!" is true and useful. burst は はじける is OK. The famous meaning quiz is a good check.
- C: seg8 does not read as English. Everything else has a good rhythm ("Ding, ding, ding!").
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Name a country. Then add "is famous for" and something special.` (A+C). seg12 say/subtitleEn "Take" -> "take" (objective, same as the "Now, point" rule). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can say "It's a beautiful country" and "Canada is famous for maple syrup."` (A+C).

## L255 Travel Agency Role-Play
- A: The travel shop role-play is natural ("How about Canada?", "That sounds fun! One ticket, please.", "Have a nice trip!"). "Ring, ring!" for a shop door is more of a phone sound in the US, but it is fine for a bell (A only). seg19 "The customer says One ticket, please." runs together. The wrap "suggest Let's go to, You can." is not a sentence.
- B: testPoints[1] "er ir ur を よみわけられる" contradicts itself: all three are the same sound, so there is nothing to tell apart (the same problem fixed in L185). Recycling where/want/you can is a good review.
- C: seg8 ("say Let's go to, or How about? Then add You can, for the fun part.") and the wrap are hard to read aloud. seg19 needs a pause before the quote.
- Decision: testPoints[1] -> "er・ir・ur の ことばを よめる（どれも ア～）" (objective+B). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `To suggest, say "Let's go to" or "How about?" Then add "You can" for the fun part.` (A+C). seg19 say/subtitleEn -> `The customer says, "One ticket, please."` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can ask "Where do you want to go?" Then suggest with "Let's go to" and "You can."` (A+C).

## L256 I Went to the Sea
- A: Back-from-summer talk is natural ("I played on the beach!", "She has a big garden."). "We saw many stars." could be "lots of stars", but it is fine (A only). seg8 "I went to, and a place." and the wrap are bare comma chains.
- B: went as the past of go is introduced simply ("Go is now. Went is before."), and "I went camping" without to is a great point. The make-it-up permission is good for privacy. The air tip is correct.
- C: The "I went to, and a place." pause is odd. "Splash, splash! ... Phew, rest time." reads well.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Say "I went to" and a place.` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can say "I went to the sea" and "I went to my grandma's house."` (A+C).

## L257 It Was Fun
- A: Showing summer photos is natural. seg2 "Is is now. Was is before." looks like a typo in the subtitle. seg8 "It was, then a feeling word." and the wrap "You can say It was fun, and The fireworks were beautiful." are comma chains.
- B: was/were is clear ("Many things? Use were."). "hot" is not really a feeling word, but it is fine at Stage 1 (B only). teacherScript "air の 2音節語も よもう！" uses a technical kanji term that kids cannot read. "Fair, y, fairy" may be read by TTS as "fair, why" (the block-wide segment pattern).
- C: "Is is now" is a stumble. 2音節語 is hard to read aloud. The comma chains break the rhythm.
- Decision: teacherScript 2音節語 -> ながい ことば (B+C). seg2 say/subtitleEn -> `"Is" is now. "Was" is before.` (A+C). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Say "It was" and a feeling word.` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can say "It was fun" and "The fireworks were beautiful."` (A+C).

## L258 How Was Your Summer?
- A: "Welcome back! How was your summer, Riko?" is exactly what a US teacher says on the first day. "new term" is a little British (US: semester), but Japanese schools have three terms, so it fits (A only). seg8 "How was your summer? asks about feelings." and seg19 "How was asks about feelings, so we answer It was." have bare phrases. "Welcome back, superstars!" at the end of the lesson is a little odd, but it echoes the theme (A only).
- B: The question/answer pair and "Did you go anywhere?" as a follow-up are a good progression. The ear tip ("Like the ear on your head!") is clear.
- C: "How was your summer? asks..." makes a question intonation in the middle of a sentence. The wrap has the comma chain.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `"How was your summer?" asks about feelings.` (A+C). seg19 say/subtitleEn -> `"How was" asks about feelings, so we answer "It was."` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can ask "How was your summer?" and answer "It was great!"` (A+C).

## L259 I Went There with My Family
- A: The sky-train talk is natural ("All aboard! Choo choo!", "Wow! How was the sea?"). seg12 "A pretend hand is OK!" is not natural English. TPR "Change gear." is more British; a US speaker says "Shift gears." (A only, and it is an item ID that keeps the phonics word gear, so I kept it).
- B: with (who) vs by (how) is clear, and the "with plane" quiz is a good contrast. The tip that bear and earth are different sounds is helpful. "It was cool." (すずしかった) after L253 "so cool" (かっこいい) could confuse a little, but the context is clear (B only).
- C: "A pretend hand is OK!" is awkward to say. The wrap has the comma chain.
- Decision: seg12 say/subtitleEn -> "Pretending is OK!" (A+C; the JA まねっこで いいよ already matches). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can say "I went to Okinawa with my family" and "I went there by plane."` (A+C).

## L260 Summer Talk
- A: The talk-show frame and "That's nice!" as a reply are natural. seg8 "When a friend talks, say That's nice!" runs together. The wrap "You can say I went to the sea. It was fun. And you can reply, That's nice!" reads as the teacher's own story without quotes. "A friendly reply is fun." is a bit flat (A only).
- B: Joining "I went to ～." and "It was ～." is the right review goal. The air vs ear contrast (mouth open vs smile) is clear, and testPoints[1] よみわけ is correct here because the sounds differ.
- C: The wrap is ambiguous out loud ("You can say I went to the sea." sounds like a statement). seg8 needs a pause before the quote.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `say, "That's nice!"` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can say, "I went to the sea. It was fun." And you can reply, "That's nice!"` (A+C).

## For the coordinator
- Block-wide pattern: almost every lesson here had a "Here's the point" line or a wrap with bare phrases in a comma chain ("Answer with I want to go to, and a country.", "You can, then the thing to do, then in and the country.", "A country, then is famous for, then the special thing.", "You can ask How was your summer, and answer It was great."). I quoted them (block rule 11). Other world 9-10 groups will likely see the same lines in the same segments (seg8, seg20).
- Block-wide pattern: a capital after a comma mid-sentence also shows up outside "Now, ..." (L254 "At the top, Take a picture."). Worth a grep for `, [A-Z][a-z]+ the` in teacher say/subtitleEn.
- Block-wide pattern: testPoints with よみわけ for spellings of the same sound (L255 "er ir ur を よみわけられる"; L185 had the same). Other review lessons with or/ore/oar or ai/ay may have it too.
