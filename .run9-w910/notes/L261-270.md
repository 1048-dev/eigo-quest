# Run 9 review notes: L261-270 (world 9, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, testPoints, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not repeat the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A? Or b?" quiz says, US praise, Ding-dong, showtime, "Now, point", "やあ、みんな！", きのう/あした about lessons), and I did not undo any of them. There are 64 patches, and all 10 lessons change (L261-L270). There are no learning item ID changes: no vocab, expression, TPR or phonics item changes, and every slide item is still an exact lesson item. Every new sentence has 8 words or fewer. The check script passes (ALL OK): the lessons are 210-230 s long with 85-88% English.

## L261 I Saw a Rainbow
- A: The picture diary scene, "I saw a shooting star over the farm!" and "Toot, toot!" are natural. The wrap "You can say I saw a rainbow, and ask What did you see." is the bare-phrase comma chain fixed in L142/L181, and the question ends with a period. "Yesterday means saw." is a simplification, but it is fine.
- B: saw = see の「みた」 is clear, and the ar/or review is right. In seg7 the answer has no speaker label, so a child hears Riko asking and then Riko answering. The JA subtitle labels it ビリモ.
- C: In seg7 the voice actor cannot tell the speaker changed. The commas in the seg20 wrap force odd pauses. "To ask, What did you see?" in seg8 is a little stiff, but it reads OK (C only).
- Decision: seg7 say/subtitleEn add "Birimo says," (B+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can say "I saw a rainbow" and ask "What did you see?"` (A+C).

## L262 I Ate Shaved Ice
- A: "Don don!" is Japanese drum onomatopoeia. An American says "Boom, boom!". "Ice, crunch, crunch, with sweet syrup on top." is not a sentence. "Sweet job!" is a playful pun, so it is OK. "What did you eat in summer?" would be "this summer" in casual talk (A only; conversation line).
- B: The quiz JA says きのうの ことだよ, but nothing in the English says yesterday (the dialogue is about summer), so the EN and JA do not match. seg9 has a sentence that starts with a lowercase "er!". The "I ate" + taste structure is clear. スイートな せいかい is a pun and is understandable (B only).
- C: "Don don" and "Ice, crunch, crunch, with" are awkward to say. The comma in "You can say I ate, and tell the taste" and in seg8 "Say I ate, then" makes odd pauses.
- Decision: seg0 "Don don!" -> "Boom, boom!" (A+C). seg3 -> "Crunchy ice with sweet syrup on top." (A+C). seg8 and seg20 quote "I ate" (A+C). seg9 -> "Three spellings, one sound, er!" (objective). seg18 say/subtitleJa きのうの -> すぎた ことだよ (objective).

## L263 I Enjoyed Swimming
- A: The pair diary practice and "They were big and loud!" are natural. "It was so fun." is ordinary kid speech in the US. "Can you hear the change?" should be "the difference" when you compare two sounds. In seg8, "I enjoyed, then a fun thing." is broken.
- B: enjoy + ed and the I -> We note are clear. The quiz distractor "I enjoy the fireworks yesterday." works as an error to spot. The wrap has a stray capital "We" mid-sentence.
- C: "Can you hear the change?" sounds odd. The seg8 fragment and the seg20 comma chain break the rhythm.
- Decision: seg8 -> `Say "I enjoyed" and a fun thing.` (A+C). seg9 change -> difference (A+C). seg20 quotes both sentences (A+C).

## L264 What Did You Do on Sunday?
- A: The Monday-morning chat, "We watch movies and play soccer." and "Popcorn, please!" are natural. TPR "Pet the dog's fur." is a little wordy ("Pet the dog."), but it is an item ID (A only). The wrap "You can ask What did you do on Sunday, and answer..." has no question mark.
- B: The did / -ed contrast and the play vs watch quiz are clear. The ar/ur minimal pairs (far/fur, barn/burn) are good.
- C: The wrap commas make an odd pause. The rest reads well.
- Decision: seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can ask "What did you do on Sunday?" and answer with played and watched.` (A+C).

## L265 My Picture Diary
- A: "I went to. I saw. I ate. I enjoyed." read as four full sentences. "Where, what you saw, what you ate." is a fragment chain. "Let's practice the show!" should be "practice for the show". "The sun goes up and down" sounds like the sun bounces. Using "Click!" for picture is fine, because picture covers photos too (A only).
- B: Joining went/saw/ate/enjoyed is good recycling. In seg7 "What great diaries!" is Ms. Emma's line, but nothing marks the speaker, so it sounds like Kiko. L263 uses "And I say,".
- C: "I went to. I saw." with full stops sounds broken aloud. seg8 is hard to phrase. In seg7 the speaker change is not marked.
- Decision: seg1 -> "I went to... I saw... I ate... I enjoyed..." with subtitleJa to match (A+C). seg5 -> "The sun comes up and goes down." (A+C). seg7 add "And I say," and JA わたし「…」 (B+C). seg8 -> `Where did you go? What did you see and eat? Then finish with "I enjoyed the day."` (A+C). seg11 -> "Let's practice for the show!" (A+C).

## L266 My Memory Speech
- A: The library scene and "Let me tell you about my summer." are natural. seg8 "Beginning, Let me tell you about. Middle, First, then Then." is not English a teacher would say. "What did you do then?" could be "next", but it is fine.
- B: The beginning/middle/end structure is a great idea, but seg8 buries it. The recap "2音節語" is a hard kanji term for kids. The tipJa already says 2つに わけて よもう. The "thirs-ty" split is phonetic rather than a dictionary split, but it is easy to say (B only).
- C: "First, then Then" is a tongue-twister. In seg1, "Then use First and Then" is confusing without quotes.
- Decision: seg1, seg8 and seg20 quote the phrases. seg8 -> `Start with "Let me tell you about." In the middle, use "First" and "Then." End with "It was a wonderful summer."` (A+C). recap[2] -> "2つに わけて よめたね" (objective+B).

## L267 Sky Memory Quiz Show
- A: The quiz show frame ("Ladies and gentlemen", "Ten points for you!", "One hundred points!") is natural and fun. "Went is past, so did." is a teacher simplification, and it is OK.
- B: Short vs long answers (Riko did! / Riko went to the zoo.) are clear. The did/does quiz fits. おねえちゃん for "sister" is a guess (it could be a younger sister), but it is fine for the scene (B only).
- C: Reads well. The wrap "and ask Who went to the zoo." has the comma/period problem.
- Decision: seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `and ask "Who went to the zoo?"` (A+C).

## L268 Speech Practice
- A: Speech tips, "Your voice was clear." and "Kind words make everyone shine." are natural. "cheer your friends with, Good job!" is unidiomatic ("cheer on" or "tell"). "cheer with Good job." in the wrap has the same problem.
- B: The three tips are concrete and the TPR matches them. The five r-vowel review is clear.
- C: "with, Good job!" has a forced pause. The slow "Hel... lo..." drill is fun to read.
- Decision: seg1 -> `And tell your friends "Good job!"` (A+C). seg20 -> `you can say "Good job!"` (A+C).

## L269 Sky Continent Review
- A: The review walk and the France/museum reasons are natural. "That's a reason." is flat as praise; "That's a good reason!" is better. The seg20 wrap "You joined I'm good at, I usually and I want to go to, with a reason." is hard to follow. TPR "Sit on your chair." ("in your chair" is also common) is fine (A only).
- B: Reason with "You can" is a good link. museum = びじゅつかん fits the context.
- C: In seg8, "After I want to go to, add a reason with You can." is hard to phrase without quotes. The seg20 chain is hard to say.
- Decision: seg8 quote the phrases (A+C). seg19 -> "That's a good reason!" and JA いい りゆうだね！ (A+C). seg20 -> `You used "I'm good at," "I usually" and "I want to go to." You added a reason, too.` (A+C).

## L270 World 9 Grand Review
- A: The show frame and "You're a World 9 champion!" are natural. "You're in Grade 6!" is not US usage ("sixth grade"). The "Grade 6 show" ID stays by course decision. seg8 "Last summer, I went, it was." is not a sentence.
- B: The usually vs past contrast is the right closing point, but seg8 hides it. "Grade 6" in a sentence teaches non-US usage. seg12 "Last, Take a bow." has a stray capital.
- C: seg8 reads like a broken quote. The rest flows well.
- Decision: seg5 -> "You're in sixth grade!" (A+B). seg8 -> `For every day, say "I usually." For last summer, say "I went" and "It was."` with subtitleJa to match (A+C). seg12 -> "Last, take a bow." (objective).

## For the coordinator

- Block-wide pattern: "Here's the point." lines that chain bare phrases with commas ("Beginning, Let me tell you about. Middle, First, then Then.", "Last summer, I went, it was.", "After I want to go to, add..."). This is block rule 11 in W07-08. I fixed these by quoting.
- Block-wide pattern: in conversation segments, a quoted answer sometimes has no speaker label, so it sounds like the first speaker (L261 seg7, L265 seg7). Worth a quick scan in other lessons.
- Block-wide pattern: mid-sentence capitals after "Last," (L270 seg12), the same as the "Now, point" rule.
