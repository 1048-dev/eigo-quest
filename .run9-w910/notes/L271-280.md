# Run 9 review notes: L271-280 (world 10, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, testPoints, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not repeat the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A? Or b?" quiz says, US praise, Ding-dong, showtime, "Now, point" lowercase, "やあ、みんな！", L274 "Give me a thumbs-up."), and I did not undo any of them. There are 41 patches in all 10 lessons (L271-L280). No lesson is unchanged. No learning item IDs change (no vocab, expression, TPR or phonics item was touched), and no slide items change. The world 10 soft c / soft g words and tips are untouched, so tests/phonics-w10.test.ts is not affected. Most patches apply one pattern: the "Here's the point" segments (seg8) explain the grammar as a comma chain ("Ask, Where do, the animal, live?"). That is not a sentence when read aloud, so I quoted the fixed parts, following block rule 11 from W07-08. Every new sentence has 8 words or fewer, except "Things, then "are bad for," then someone." and similar formula lines, which are 7-8 words. The check script passes (ALL OK): the lessons are 217-227 s long with 87-89% English.

## L271 Where Do Sea Turtles Live?
- A: The aquarium opening, "They swim very far!", "I read it in a book." and Birimo's "Where do you live, little fish?" are natural. seg8 "Ask, Where do, the animal, live? Answer, They live in, and the place." is board shorthand, not what a teacher says. The vocab "earth" is lowercase for the planet (OK as "the earth", item ID).
- B: The level is right. "they は その いきものたち" is a good gloss. The soft c rule (e, i, y) is correct and ice/city/rice/pencil fit it. The wrap "You can ask Where do sea turtles live, and answer..." has no quotes, but the phrases are full sentences, so it reads OK.
- C: seg8 forces odd pauses around "Where do" and "live". "C often says k" / "C says s": TTS may read k and s as letter names (block pattern, see below).
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Ask, "Where do," then the animal, then "live?" Answer, "They live in," then the place.` (A+C, block rule 11).

## L272 Animals and Their Homes
- A: The animal-map scene, "Koalas live in trees. They sleep a lot!" and "A camel in the snow? Brrr!" are natural. seg8 "Animal, live in, place." is shorthand.
- B: The plural note (どうぶつの なまえに s) is useful. The ce ending tip (final e is silent) is correct. seg17 says "Say, They live in..." but the subtitleJa shows the slide pattern 「～ live in ～.」, so EN and JA do not match.
- C: seg8 reads as three loose words. "Don't walk away. Just dance right there!" is easy to say.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Animals, then "live in," then the place.` (A+C). seg17 subtitleJa -> "They live in … と いってね" (objective, EN/JA mismatch; the slide item stays).

## L273 What Do Whales Eat?
- A: Feeding time, "Some whales eat tiny shrimp." and "Mice racing in space? Zoom!" are fine. seg19 "What do, so we answer with eat." is not a sentence. seg20 pairs "What do whales eat" with "They eat bamboo", which is wrong. TPR "Look up at space." is a little odd ("the sky" is more usual), but it is an item (A only).
- B: The Where/What contrast note is helpful. The quiz distractor "They live in bamboo." tests the right thing. seg19 has to explain why, and right now it doesn't. seg20 teaches the wrong fact (whales do not eat bamboo).
- C: seg8 "Ask with What do, the animal, and eat." and seg19 "What do, so..." both trip the reader.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Ask, "What do," then the animal, then "eat?" Answer, "They eat," then the food.` (A+C). seg19 say/subtitleEn -> `The question has "eat," so we answer with "eat."` (A+B+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa "whales" -> "pandas" (objective, wrong meaning).

## L274 Animals in Danger
- A: The poster talk, "Turtles think the bags are jellyfish." and "A fancy prince picks up trash!" are natural. seg8 "Animals, are in danger. Something, is bad for, someone." is shorthand. seg17 "Say, ... are in danger." is read as "Say, are in danger."
- B: The message (we can help) fits the age. seg8 says "is bad for", but the taught pattern is "are bad for" (objective). "cent" is in the word list, tipJa and recap but is never said in the script (B only, kept; it may be on purpose to avoid cent/sent). きき in testPoints/aiTask is correct but hard (B only).
- C: seg8 and seg17 both have the bare-ellipsis or comma-chain problem. The rest reads well.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Animals, then "are in danger." Things, then "are bad for," then someone.` (A+C+objective). seg17 say/subtitleEn -> `Say its name, then "are in danger."` (A+C).

## L275 Sea Creature Quiz
- A: The quiz show, "No peeking!" and "Dolphins can't walk at all." are fun and natural. seg6 "I give the first quiz." sounds translated ("give a quiz" means give a test). seg1 and seg20 chain bare phrases ("Give hints with They live in and They eat.").
- B: The c review (s vs k by the next letter) is clear, and testPoints "よみわけられる" is right here (two different sounds). "They ... It's a dolphin" (they/it) is fine for species talk.
- C: In seg1 and seg20, "They live in and They eat" runs together. "My quiz is first." is easier to say.
- Decision: seg1 and seg20 say/subtitleEn quote the phrases (`"They live in" and "They eat,"`, `ask, "What animal is it?"`) (A+C, same as the L181 fix). seg6 say/subtitleEn "I give the first quiz." -> "My quiz is first." (A+C).

## L276 Where Is This Banana From?
- A: The harbor market, the stickers and "She knows it's a banana. She wants the place!" are natural. "every food has a little sticker" is slightly odd (A only). seg8 is the comma chain again.
- B: The soft g rule and giant/page/gem/cage are correct. The quiz distractor "It's a banana." is a good check. seg0 subtitleJa has a spacing error: 「くだもの が」.
- C: seg8 "Ask, Where is, the food, from?" needs pauses that don't match the meaning.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Ask, "Where is," then the food, then "from?" Answer, "It's from," then the place.` (A+C). seg0 subtitleJa 「くだもの が」 -> 「くだものが」 (objective).

## L277 It's from Hokkaido
- A: The Japan food map, "They are from Ehime. Many oranges grow there." and "A huge orange dances on the stage!" are fine. seg8 "One thing, use is." is clipped, and "for one thing" is an idiom.
- B: The is/are point is useful, and the quiz with "These apples is from Aomori." tests it well. The ge tip is correct. "They are huge!" could be "They're" (info only).
- C: "One thing, use is. ... Many things, use are." is hard to read with the right stress.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `One thing? Use "is." ... Many things? Use "are."` (A+C).

## L278 What's in Our School Lunch?
- A: Curry day, "Itadakimasu!" / "Gochisousama!" and "A giraffe does magic in the gym!" are fine. "Make strong arms!" is not idiomatic ("Make a muscle!"). "Up, up, tall neck!": a neck is long, not tall. seg8 "What's in, the dish?" is shorthand. "lunch time" (lunchtime) and TPR "Jog in place in the gym." are OK (A only; the TPR is an item).
- B: "Make a muscle!" matches the JA ちからこぶを つくって. seg19 "What's in means what food is inside." is hard to follow. seg7 「おかわりは ひとつ まで」 is unnatural Japanese (おかわり is counted 1かい).
- C: seg8 and seg19 need quotes to be read well. "tall neck" sounds wrong.
- Decision: seg5 -> "Make a muscle!" (A+B). seg7 say/subtitleJa -> 「おかわりは 1かい までね」 (objective). seg8 -> `Ask, "What's in," then the dish.` (A+C). seg12 "tall neck" -> "long neck" (A+objective, EN/JA). seg19 -> `"What's in" asks what's inside.` (A+C). Expression "Carrots, onions and potatoes." (no serial comma) is an item and is kept.

## For the coordinator
- Block-wide pattern: the seg8 "Here's the point" lines in world 10 (and probably world 9) explain grammar as comma chains ("Ask, Where do, the animal, live?", "Animal, live in, place.", "Something, is bad for, someone.", "One thing, use is."). I used one shape for all of them: `Ask, "Where do," then the animal, then "live?"`. Please apply the same shape in the other groups so the course stays consistent.
- Block-wide pattern: "Your turn" lines that start with a bare ellipsis ("Say, ... are in danger.", "Say, ... eat ...") are read by TTS as "Say, are in danger." I changed them to "Say its name, then "are in danger."" and similar. Lines that start with a word ("Say, They live in...", "Say, This... is from...") are fine and were kept.
- Block-wide pattern: "C says s / k" and "G says j / g" (seg9, seg10 in every lesson, plus "S or K? ... City, s!" in L275 and "J or G? ... Giant, j!" in L280). TTS may read these as letter names (ess, kay, jay, gee). This is the same risk as the segment drills. I did not change it, because it is in every W10 phonics segment. If the block uses "/s/" for sounds (as in L188 "/kw/"), it should be done everywhere at once.
- The subtitleJa lines repeat the Japanese part of say at the end, which sometimes gives doubles ("いっしょに よもう。いっしょに ポスターを よもう。" L274 seg0; "dge も「ジ」！ dge も「ジ」だよ。" L279 seg9). This is the product design, so I kept it.
