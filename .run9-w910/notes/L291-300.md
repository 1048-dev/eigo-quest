# Run 9 review notes: L291-300 (world 10, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, testPoints, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not repeat the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A? Or b?" quiz says, US praise, Ding-dong, showtime, "Now, point", "やあ、みんな！", L292/L298 autumn -> faucet/launch), and I did not undo any of them. The faucet and launch changes read well: "faucet は じゃぐち" is a helpful gloss, and "Draw a rocket launch on the lawn. A bird flew by!" is easy to say. There are 65 patches, and all 10 lessons change (L291-L300). No learning item IDs change: no vocab, expression, TPR or phonics item is touched. The only item-like change is the L295 conversation line 3 ("We can pick up trash at the beach every month." -> "We can clean the beach every month."). Its seg7 say, subtitleEn, subtitleJa and slide item are updated too. No test in tests/ or code in src/ uses that string. Every new sentence has 8 words or fewer. The check script passes (ALL OK): the lessons are 209-226 s long with 88-89% English.

## L291 We Can Save the Sea
- A: The beach clean-up talk, "We can help!" and "You're a beach hero!" are natural. seg8 "We can plus an action tells what we are able to do." has an unquoted phrase as its subject and reads like a broken sentence. The wrap "You can say We can pick up trash, and Let's clean the beach." is the comma-chain pattern fixed in L142/L181.
- B: The level is right, and the We can vs Let's contrast in the quiz is clear. "are able to" is above Stage 1 when "can" is the point. The aw tip (open your mouth tall) is correct. vocab beach = すなはま while the expressions say ビーチ: it is a small mismatch but fine (B only).
- C: In seg1 "with We can, and invite" the comma makes an odd pause, and the same happens in seg8 and seg20. "Slurp, slurp!", "Swish, swish!" and "Meow!" read well.
- Decision: seg1, seg8 and seg20 say/subtitleEn quote the patterns (A+C). seg8 also uses "what we can do" instead of "what we are able to do" (A+B+C).

## L292 Let's Save Water
- A: "Don't leave the water on." and "Oops!" are natural. seg8 "Don't leave the water on means, please turn it off!" does not read as one sentence. "Rocket launch! Three, two, one, go!" is fine ("blast off" is more typical, but "go" is OK). The line "We can turn off the water when we brush our teeth." (11 words) is natural, and the when-clause is the whole scene.
- B: The 11-word line is over the Stage 1 maximum, but the target "We can turn off the water" comes first and the clause recycles "Brush your teeth" from the TPR. au = aw is taught clearly, and the faucet gloss helps. seg12 "And... Pause." breaks the block rule (lowercase after "..." when the sentence goes on).
- C: The 11-word line is one easy breath, so I kept it. The comma after "means" in seg8 trips the reader, and so does the seg20 comma chain.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `"Don't leave the water on" means "Please turn it off!"` (A+C). seg12 "And... pause." (objective, block rule). seg20 quotes the phrases (A+C). The 11-word conversation line is kept (B only).

## L297 Earth Quiz Show

- B: seg8 should give each pattern as a full question, like the JA subtitle does. The -y (ee/eye) and -le review is correct. In seg4 the JA "あはっ！" is a laugh, while "Aha!" means "I get it": that is an EN/JA mismatch.

- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `For animals, ask "Where do they live?" For food, ask "Where is it from?" For how people live, say "In Japan, people bow."` (A+B+C). The JA subtitle already matches. seg4 subtitleJa あはっ！ -> そうか！ (objective).
