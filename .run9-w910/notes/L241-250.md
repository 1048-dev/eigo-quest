# Run 9 review notes: L241-250 (world 9, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, testPoints, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not repeat the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, US praise, "やあ、みんな！", "Now, point", "まえの レッスン"), and I did not undo any of them. There are 66 patches on 66 fields, and all 10 lessons change (L241-L250). As in L251-260, most patches are the "Here's the point" lines (seg8) and the wraps (seg20) that list bare phrases in a comma chain ("You can say I'm good at, and ask What are you good at.", "My favorite, then food, color, or sport. Then is."). I quoted them, following block rule 11 and the L142 fix. Other fixes: one quiz that cannot be answered by ear (L244 "Me, too!" vs "Me, two!"), one English "Yesterday" left behind when block rule 12 fixed only the Japanese (L247), a British/Australian "Good day" (L244), two testPoints, and a few unnatural lines. No learning item IDs change (no vocab, expression, TPR or phonics strings), and no slide items change. The check script passes (ALL OK): the lessons are 210-218 s long with 84-87% English. Lint: error 0 / warn 1 (the L248 conversation line, kept; see below).

## L241 This Is Me!
- A: The first-day card making is natural ("Welcome to the Sky Continent! Let's make This Is Me cards.", "I'm good at flying! Look at me!"). seg8 "After good at, use ing." and the wrap "You can say I'm good at, and ask What are you good at." are bare comma chains. seg0 "Today we make This Is Me cards!" could be "we're making", but it is fine for a teacher (A only).
- B: "good at + -ing" is a clear single point, and the draw/drawing quiz checks exactly that. The ar tip (open wide, then curl the tongue) is correct. continent is glossed (たいりく). The nickname note in aiTask protects privacy.
- C: seg8 "After good at, use ing" and the wrap force odd pauses at the commas. The blending segment and the speed challenge read well.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `After "good at," use "ing."` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can say "I'm good at" and ask "What are you good at?"` (A+C).

## L242 My Favorite Things
- A: The garden talk is natural ("What's yours, Haru?", "Like the grass in this yard!"). seg1 "Tell your number one things." is not English a teacher would say (tell needs a listener; "number one things" is a calque). seg8 "My favorite, then food, color, or sport. Then is." is a comma chain. The wrap "You can tell your favorite food, color, and sport." also lacks a listener. "My favorite thing is the stars at night!" agrees with its subject, so it stays.
- B: "My favorite + category + is" is broken down well, and the sport/color quiz is a good meaning check. The tip contrasting short a (cat) and ar is helpful. seg20 "tell your favorite" is a model kids may copy.
- C: seg1 and seg8 are hard to say smoothly ("Then is." is a stumble). The rest reads well.
- Decision: seg1 say/subtitleEn "Tell your number one things." -> "Say what you like best." (A+C; the JA いちばん すきな ものを つたえよう still matches). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Say "My favorite" and food, color, or sport. Then add "is."` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn "tell" -> "say" (A+B).

## L244 What's Your Favorite Animal?
- A: seg0 "Good day, everyone!" is British/Australian; an American teacher says "Hello". seg8 "To ask, What's your favorite animal? To answer, My favorite animal is. Same thing?" has a broken fragment ("My favorite animal is."). The quiz "A, Me, too! Or B, Me, two!" sounds identical out loud. The wrap is a comma chain. Riko repeats "What's your favorite animal?" instead of "What's yours?", but repeating the target is the point.
- B: The too/two quiz is a spelling check, but the teacher only says it, and seg19 praises "Great listening!". That is objectively wrong: nothing can be heard. Also seg19 say is missing "Tricky!", which the subtitleEn and JA (ひっかけ だったね) have. The two-part syllable tip (gar-den, mar-ket) is good.
- C: seg8 and the wrap need quotes to be read as questions and answers. "Good day" sounds out of character.
- Decision: seg0 say/subtitleEn -> "Hello, everyone!" (A+C). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `To ask, say "What's your favorite animal?" To answer, say "My favorite animal is..." Same thing?` (A+C). seg18 say/subtitleEn add "Look at the spelling." and subtitleJa add つづりを みてね。 (A+B+C, objective). seg19 say "Great listening!" -> "Tricky! Good eye!", subtitleEn "Great listening!" -> "Good eye!", subtitleJa よく きけたね！ -> よく みてたね！ (A+B+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can ask "What's your favorite animal?" and say "Me, too!"` (A+C).

## L245 This Is Me Cards
- A: The card exchange is natural ("What wonderful cards, everyone!"). seg8 "Name, good at, favorite. Then, Here is my card." is a comma chain. The wrap "You can tell your name, what you're good at, and your favorite." has no noun after "favorite". "Here's my card" would be more casual, but "Here is my card." is the expression ID (A only). aiTask "Tell me about you!" is colloquial and fine (A only). seg4 "I give you my card, you give me yours." is a spoken comma splice, fine (A only).
- B: testPoints[1] "ar を よみわけられる" says to tell apart, but this lesson has only one sound (the same problem as L255 and L185). Putting three patterns together is the right review goal.
- C: seg8 does not read as English. The wrap trails off at "your favorite".
- Decision: testPoints[1] -> "ar（アー）の ことばを よめる" (objective+B). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Name first. Then "good at." Then "favorite." Last, say "Here is my card."` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> "You can say your name, what you're good at, and your favorite things." (A+B).

## L246 What Time Do You Get Up?
- A: The morning-time talk is natural ("I'm sleepy in the morning!", "I like the morning sky!"). seg8 "Before a time, use at." and the wrap "answer with at" have a bare "at". seg4 "half past six" is understood in the US, and "six thirty" is the model (A only). The quiz option "I get up in five." is real English with another meaning, which is fine for a distractor.
- B: "at + time" is one clear point, and the at/in quiz checks it. The or tip (round mouth, curl tongue) is correct. The "not your real time is OK" note is good.
- C: "use at" and "answer with at" swallow the word "at". "Brrring!" and "Giddy up!" read well.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Before a time, use "at."` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can ask "What time do you get up?" and answer with "at."` (A+C).

## L250 Schools Around the World
- A: Comparing schools is natural ("First stop, Australia!", "Usually around nine."). seg8 lists bare patterns. seg12 "Lunch time." should be "Lunchtime.". The wrap "tell about schools in Japan" is not natural ("talk about"). The quiz "Or B, or, like corn." is confusing in the subtitle.
- B: In/we vs students is a clear contrast, and the ar/or listening contrast is a good review. testPoints[1] "ar or を よみわけられる" reads as one word; here the sounds really differ, so よみわけ is right.
- C: "Or B, or" is hard to read; quotes help the voice mark the sound names. "tell about" is a stumble.
- Decision: testPoints[1] -> "ar と or を よみわけられる" (B+C). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `For other countries, say "In Australia, students..." For us, say "In Japan, we..."` (A+C). seg12 say/subtitleEn "Lunch time." -> "Lunchtime." (objective). seg18 say/subtitleEn -> `A, "ar," like car. Or B, "or," like corn.` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn "tell about" -> "talk about" (A+B).
