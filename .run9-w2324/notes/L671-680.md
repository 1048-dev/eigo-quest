#### L671 If You Heat Ice
- A: The ice / water / steam demo is natural. The water poster opens "Water can change its shape." A US science teacher says form or state; liquid water changes shape all the time, so "shape" is the wrong idea. "Water is a great shape changer!" repeats it. "If you heat water to 100 degrees" reads as Fahrenheit to an American. The AI starter has Kiko owning a hot plate.
- B: Same flag on shape vs state, because this is the first lesson on states of matter. The rule line "If you heat, it melts." drops the object of heat (it is ungrammatical as a model). In the starter, a child with her own hot plate goes against the L662 safety rules and the tested "An adult turns on the hot plate." The JA ゆげ（すいじょうき）is fine. Steam as "a little white cloud" (seg4) is the everyday meaning, which is fine for kids (single flag, kept).
- C: Reads well. TTS may spell out capital syllables like "PHO" and "TOG" in seg9 (see block-wide notes). The quiz options "A, the lid is crying. B, If you cool steam..." mix lower and upper case, but this is not heard (single flag, kept).
- Decision: teacherScript: "change its shape" -> "change its form", "100 degrees" -> "100 degrees Celsius", "Water is a great shape changer!" -> "Water can change a lot!", "If you heat, it melts." -> "If you heat ice, it melts." (objective). starter -> "Dr. Moss has ice cubes and a hot plate." (A+B).
