### Run 9 review notes: L191-200 (world 7, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A? Or b?" says, "He can... or she can...", timetable -> schedule, US praise, "Give a thumbs-up."). There are 45 patches in 8 lessons (L191, L192, L193, L194, L195, L196, L197, L200). L198 and L199 have no changes. No learning item IDs change (no vocab, expression, TPR or phonics text was touched). The most common fix is the "formula read aloud as a comma chain" pattern in "Here's the point" / "Your turn" segments ("Do you have, a subject, on, a day?", "I have, and, on Friday."). I replaced these with model sentences or "..." pauses, and the subtitleJa keeps the ＋ formula. Every new English sentence has 8 words or fewer. The check script passes: ALL OK. The touched lessons are 205-223 s long with 81-84% English, and every slide item is valid.

#### L191 School Subjects
- A: The subject talk is natural ("I like P.E. I like running.", "I like sea animals!"). "Close your textbook. Pat!": no US teacher uses "Pat!" for a book closing. "Snap!" or "Thump!" is used. "She uses and." sounds unfinished.
- B: Level and progression are good. The ea /iː/ tip is correct, and "read" is the present tense here. EN and JA match. Two subjects + and is taught clearly in the Japanese.
- C: TTS will read "Pat!" as the name Pat. "She uses and." ends on a function word, so the voice drops and it sounds cut off.
- Decision: seg12 say/subtitleEn "Pat!" -> "Snap!" (A+C). seg7 say/subtitleEn -> `She uses "and."` (A+C). JA パタン is kept.

#### L192 What Do You Have on Monday?
- A: "What do you have on Monday?" and "Science is fun!" are natural. After the change, "Schedule. Days go across, subjects go down." still describes a Japanese 時間割 well, and the JA よこに ようび、たてに きょうか matches. In seg7, "Ask with What do you have, then on and a day." is not how a teacher talks.
- B: The seg7 rule is the key point of the lesson, but the English form is hard to follow. A full model question plus "Say on before the day." is clearer. The JA explanation still matches.
- C: The seg7 comma chain cannot be phrased naturally. The rest reads well ("La, la, la! Clap to the beat.").
- Decision: seg7 say/subtitleEn -> "Ask, What do you have on Monday? Say on before the day. ..." (A+B+C).

#### L193 I Have Math and Art
- A: In seg0, "Ding-dong!" is a doorbell, but Riko is calling on a tablet, and a call goes "Ring, ring!". The seg1 goal starts "Today's goal. I have math and art on Wednesday." with no "Say,", so it sounds like Ms. Emma's own schedule. "Brilliant thinking!" is British praise that the block pass missed.
- B: Same flag on the goal line. The JA ピンポーン is also a doorbell, which does not fit でんわ. In seg8, "Do you have, a subject, on, a day?" and in seg17, "I have, and, on Wednesday." are formulas read aloud, so children will copy the commas. The Wednesday silent-d tip is good.
- C: The seg8 and seg17 comma chains are unreadable. "Tongue between your teeth. Th, th, Thursday!" is fine.
- Decision: seg0 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa -> "Ring, ring!" / プルルル！ (A+B). seg1 say/subtitleEn add "Say," / "Then ask," (A+B+C). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "Do you have music on Thursday?" (A+C). seg17 say/subtitleEn -> "I have... and... on Wednesday." (A+C). seg19 "Brilliant thinking!" -> "Great thinking!" (objective, block rule).

#### L194 My Favorite Subject
- A: The lunch talk and Birimo's joke ("My favorite subject is lunch!") are natural and fun. "Lunch is not a subject!" is kept uncontracted on purpose, for emphasis. In seg4, "Fun. Ha ha!" acts out funny, not fun.
- B: fun vs funny is a known confusion (style guide 6), and L200 uses "Ha ha ha!" for funny. "Yay!" separates the two. In seg8, "My favorite subject is, and one subject." is a formula read aloud.
- C: The seg8 comma chain is awkward. "I teach, you speak!" is a nice line.
- Decision: seg4 say/subtitleEn "Ha ha!" -> "Yay!", subtitleJa あはは -> わーい (A+B). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "Say, My favorite subject is art. Then add a reason." (A+C).

#### L195 My Dream Schedule
- A: After the change, "Hello, schedule designers!" is unusual but fine and playful, and it matches じかんわりの デザイナーさん. "I like your schedules!" is natural. "Week. Monday to Sunday." fits a Japanese school week. In seg8, "Then, I have, a subject, on, a day." is a formula chain.
- B: In seg6, "I have soccer on Thursday!" has no speaker, so it sounds like Riko answers her own question (subtitleJa says ハルくん). The see/sea and meet/meat homophone point is correct and well explained. The seg17 "I have, and, on Friday." is a chain.
- C: The seg8 and seg17 chains are unreadable. Without "Haru says," the voice cannot show a change of speaker in seg6.
- Decision: seg6 say/subtitleEn add "Haru says," (B+C). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "First, show it. This is my dream schedule. Then say the days. I have music on Friday." (A+C). seg17 say/subtitleEn -> "I have... and... on Friday." (A+C).

#### L196 Can You Swim?
- A: "Ahoy, sailors!" and "But I can row a boat!" are natural. "Make a strong arm." is a calque; US kids say "Make a muscle." "Splendid!" is formal and British-leaning.
- B: "Make a muscle" matches ちからこぶを つくって exactly. The seg8 "Can you, and an action? ... No, I can't is OK!" is confusing. The positive message (できなくても だいじょうぶ) is good and stays. The oa tip is correct. "Splendid" is not in the style-guide praise list.
- C: "No, I can't is OK!" is very hard to say naturally. "A goat in a boat!" is fun.
- Decision: seg5 say/subtitleEn -> "Make a muscle." (A+B). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "Can you swim? Yes, I can. No, I can't. That's OK! ..." (A+C). seg20 "Splendid!" -> "Awesome!" (A+B).

#### L197 He Can Run Fast
- A: The introduction dialogue is natural ("Wow! Can he swim?", "Thank you, Riko!"). A whistle goes "Tweet!", not "Beep!". The seg7 line "This is, and a name. Then, He can, and an action. For He can't, say it kindly, and add a can!" is not teacher talk.
- B: The kindness point is valuable, but "add a can" is unclear. Model sentences from the dialogue ("He can't swim, but he can jump high.") teach the same thing directly and match the subtitleJa.
- C: The seg7 chain cannot be read. "Beep!" sounds like a machine.
- Decision: seg4 say/subtitleEn "Beep!" -> "Tweet!" (A+C). seg7 say/subtitleEn -> "This is Haru. He can run fast. He can't swim, but he can jump high. Be kind!" (A+B+C).

#### L200 This Is My Friend
- A: The show and the intros are natural. Three lines are comma chains: seg1 "This is, he or she can, and good at.", seg8 "One, This is, and a name. Two, ... Three, good at, or kind or funny." and the fragments in seg19, "so she. Haru and Birimo are he.".
- B: Same flags. A review lesson is clearer with three model sentences ("This is Kiko. She can dance well. She is kind." is the teacherScript model). subtitleJa seg1 had to follow the new English. The seg8 subtitleJa keeps the 1-2-3 formula. The oa/ea contrast is correct.
- C: The chains and "so she." cannot be read naturally.
- Decision: seg1 say/subtitleEn/subtitleJa -> "This is Kiko. She can dance well." (A+C). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "Three sentences. This is Kiko. She can dance well. She is kind." (A+C). seg19 say/subtitleEn -> "so we say she. For Haru and Birimo, we say he." (A+C).
