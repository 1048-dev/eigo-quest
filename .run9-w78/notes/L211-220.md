### Run 9 review notes: L211-220 (world 8, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A? Or b?" quiz says, US praise, "やあ、みんな！"). There are 38 patches in 9 lessons (L212-L220). L211 has no changes. No learning item IDs change: no vocab, expression, TPR or phonics text was touched. The phonics sound claims are correct in all 10 lessons: long oo moon/zoo/food/pool/spoon/room/boot/cool, short oo book/look/cook/good/foot/wood/hook/wool (all in OO_SHORT_WORDS), and ou /aʊ/ in every ou word. Every new or changed sentence has 8 words or fewer. The lint warning on L214 conversation.lines[3] is fixed. The check script prints ALL OK: the touched lessons are 210-223 s long with 84-86% English.

#### L211 Where Is the Station?
- A: The lost-traveler scene is natural ("Excuse me. I'm lost.", "It's over there!", "Let's go together!"). "Choo choo!" and "Freeze!" are real classroom talk.
- B: The level is right. One new pattern (Where is the ~?) is recycled many times. The oo /uː/ claim and the round-lips tip are correct, and EN and JA match.
- C: The script reads well. "m, oo, n" uses the block-wide letter-segment pattern (TTS may say letter names), which is not specific to this lesson.
- Decision: no change.

#### L212 At the Crossroads
- A: Birimo's "This is right!" sounds like "This is correct!". The quiz line "go the way of his right hand" is a calque of みぎての ほう. "Add at and a place." cannot be followed when it is spoken.
- B: The pun on right (correct / みぎ) blurs the target word in the very lesson that teaches it. The quiz calque is the same issue. The L trick (left hand, thumb and index finger) is correct.
- C: "Add at and a place" and "the way of his right hand" are hard to phrase aloud.
- Decision: conversation line 3 and seg7 say/subtitleEn/slide -> "Wait! That's left! Right is this way!" (A+B). seg8 quotes "at" (A+C). seg18 -> "Birimo wants to go to the right." (A+B+C).

#### L213 It's on Your Right
- A: The town words and "Look! A cookbook! It looks good!" are natural. US stamps are mostly stickers now, but the stamp joke is fine.
- B: In seg3, the JA is a question (きってを なめる？), but the EN gives an order ("Lick the stamp."). The short-oo vs long-oo contrast (moon vs book) is clear and correct.
- C: The joke "Lick the stamp. Eww, no!" only works with question intonation.
- Decision: seg3 say/subtitleEn -> "Lick the stamp? Eww, no!" (B+C).

#### L214 Next to the Park
- A: Ms. Emma's read-back "Next to the park, between the park and the bookstore." repeats "the park" in a way nobody would say. "Between needs two places and the word and." is hard to follow. "Yummy job!" is playful, not standard, but it fits the cafe theme.
- B: The read-back has 10 words, over the Stage 1 maximum (lint warn). The quiz setup sentence has 13 words. The prepositions are taught one at a time with good gestures.
- C: The read-back and the quiz setup are each too long for one breath, and "...the word and." ends on a word that cannot be stressed naturally.
- Decision: conversation line 3 (en+ja) and seg7 say/subtitleJa/subtitleEn/slide -> "Between the park and the bookstore. Got it!" (A+B+C). seg8 quotes "Between" / "and" (A+C). seg18 setup -> "Look! The park, the cafe, the bookstore. The cafe is in the middle." with matching JA (B+C).

#### L215 Help the Traveler
- A: The review flow and "You're welcome. Have a nice trip!" are natural. "Ding dong!" is normally written "Ding-dong!".
- B: The review lesson is a good one. It links direction and place, then contrasts long and short oo ("Food on my foot?"). EN and JA match.
- C: The script reads well. "Ding dong!" without a hyphen gives TTS an odd pause.
- Decision: seg0 say/subtitleEn "Ding dong!" -> "Ding-dong!" (objective, the same rule as W03-06).

#### L216 What Would You Like?
- A: The ordering dialogue is natural. "And you, Ms. Emma?" is fine from a child playing a waiter (a real server says "And for you?"). In seg8, "I want is OK at home." cannot be parsed when spoken. In the wrap, "order with I'd like, please" sounds like a request.
- B: The I want / I'd like contrast is a good teaching point, and the HAM stress note is correct. ou /aʊ/ and "Ow!" for a stubbed toe are correct.
- C: The seg8 phrase list and the comma chain in the wrap are hard to read aloud.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn quote "I want" / "I'd like." / "please" (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can ask "What would you like?" You can say "I'd like a hamburger, please."` (A+C, the same as the L142 wrap fix).

#### L217 I'd Like Spaghetti
- A: Natural. Kiko asks "Anything else?" after Haru's "That's all.", but a server offering dessert after that is realistic. "Delicious work!" is playful. In seg8, "For drinks, add to drink." and "say That's all, or No, thank you." are hard to follow.
- B: The to drink rule is clear. The JA for the drink answer drops おねがいします (オレンジジュース。), but that is natural and consistent. The tip "おわりの nd" fits 3 of the 4 words (mouth ends in th). This is minor.
- C: The seg8 phrases need quotes to read cleanly. "Round, n, d!" makes TTS say the letter names "en, dee" (block-wide pattern).
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn quote "to drink." / "That's all." / "No, thank you." (A+C).

#### L218 How Much Is It?
- A: The food stand scene, "Coins and bills" (US) and the 3-yen joke are good. In seg17, "Make your own price." is not natural. A US teacher says "Pick a price." or "You choose the price."
- B: Same flag on seg17. In recap[1], "500 は five hundred yen" equates a bare number with "yen". The near-duplicate prices (500 / 800 / 300 yen) are intended practice.
- C: "You are the shop owner." reads better contracted. Everything else reads well.
- Decision: seg17 say/subtitleEn -> "You're the shop owner. Pick a price." (A+B). recap[1] -> "500えんは five hundred yen" (objective+B).

#### L219 Here You Are
- A: The register scene is natural ("Your total is 1,000 yen.", "Here's your bag."). "Give and take things politely" is close to the idiom "give and take", but it is understandable. I kept it.
- B: The Japanese for Haru's "Anything else?" is ほかに ございますか？. That is misused keigo for asking the customer, and it is stiff for kids. L217 uses ほかには？. The ou /aʊ/ claim and "ch" ending note are correct.
- C: "Ch, ch, like a train!" may be read as letter names by TTS (block-wide pattern). Everything else reads well.
- Decision: conversation line 0 ja and seg6 subtitleJa -> ほかに ありますか？ (objective+B).

#### L220 Restaurant Role-Play
- A: The role-play is natural ("Enjoy your meal!", "You're a star waiter!"). In seg8, "The order is order, price, pay." mixes the two meanings of order. "Curry and rice" is the Japanese textbook name (Americans say "curry"), but it is an item ID.
- B: Same flag on seg8, and "I'd like." on its own is not a sentence. In seg9, "O, O says oo or u." uses the letter "u" for the book sound, which is wrong (TTS will say "you").
- C: "The order is order" makes the reader stumble. In "oo or u", TTS reads u as /juː/.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> "Order, ask the price, then pay. I'd like... How much is it? Here you are." (A+B+C). seg9 say/subtitleEn -> "O, O has two sounds. Moon, book." (B+C).
