### Run 9 review notes: L221-230 (world 8, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A? Or b?" quiz says, "He is... or she is...", US praise, "Give a thumbs-up."). There are 54 patches in all 10 lessons (L221-L230). **No learning item IDs change** (no vocab, expression, TPR or phonics text was touched). The only item-like change is the L226 conversation line "My hero is ... me!" -> "My hero is... me!" (spacing), with its slide item. All phonics sound claims (ow = /aʊ/ in L221-224, ou/ow in L225, oi/oy = /ɔɪ/ in L226-230, "voice の c は ス", "おわりは oy、なかは oi") are correct, so no phonics changes and no vowelTeams/tests impact. The check script prints ALL OK: every lesson is 201-228 s with 83-85% English.

#### L221 In Japan, We Have Sushi
- A: The video call with Lily is natural ("Ring, ring!", `Lily says, "How nice!"`). In seg8, "In Japan, we have, then one great thing." does not sound like a sentence. In seg19, "We have first, then the thing." sounds like "we have (something) first". "We have many hot springs." is a bit bookish (US: lots of), but it is an expression item and is correct.
- B: The level is right and "we = わたしたち" is clear. The ow /aʊ/ vs snow /oʊ/ contrast is correct, and so is the mouth tip (open, then round). Same flag on seg19: the rule is that "We have" comes before the thing, so "We have" must be the subject.
- C: seg8 "we have, then" forces a strange pause. "Ow, my toe!" and "A cow says wow!" are fun to read. The letter-by-letter blends ("c, ow, cow") have the block-wide TTS letter-name risk.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `"In Japan, we have" plus one great thing.` (A+C). seg19 say/subtitleEn -> `"We have" comes first, then the thing.` (A+C).

#### L222 You Can See Mount Fuji
- A: The poster scene works ("Grab your crayons!", "your ramen is bigger than Mount Fuji!"). seg8 "You can, then an action word." has the same broken-sentence problem as L221. "Give me a tip." and "give tips with You can" are a little unusual but fine.
- B: You can = おすすめ is explained clearly. The four ow words all end in n, and the tip says so correctly. EN and JA match. The seg0 subtitleJa begins "みんな やあ！", the space variant that the block rule (やあ、みんな！) did not catch.
- C: seg8 "You can, then" is hard to read. "Slurp, slurp!" and "Knees up, step, step!" have a good rhythm.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `"You can" plus an action word.` (A+C). seg0 subtitleJa -> やあ、みんな！ (objective, block rule).

#### L223 Festivals in Japan
- A: The festival facts are right (Tanabata in July, a snow festival in February, summer fireworks). seg8 "We have the festival, in the month. ... Then, You can enjoy the fun thing." is not English. seg19 "What a firework of an answer!" is not idiomatic. In seg5, "Count with me. Seven!" asks the children to count but gives only one number.
- B: "Months use in" and the in/on quiz are good. Same flags on seg8 (the rule is not clear) and seg5 (just say July is month number seven). seg0 has a 14-word run-on sentence.
- C: seg0 is too long to read in one breath. seg8 has broken phrasing. "What a firework of an answer" is clumsy to say.
- Decision: seg0 split into two sentences (B+C). seg5 -> "It's month number seven!" + JA 7ばんめの つき (A+B). seg8 -> "We have Tanabata in July. Use in with months. Then say what's fun. You can enjoy fireworks." + JA (A+B+C). seg19 -> "Boom! Your answer sparkles!" + JA ドーン！ キラキラの こたえ！ (A+C).

#### L224 It's Delicious
- A: The food fair is natural ("My tummy is growling!", "Try takoyaki."). "Very, very yummy!" is real child-directed US talk, so I kept it (lint stiff-phrase info). "Delicious work!" / "Yummy job!" are playful puns and fine. The quiz distractor "Mochi is hot spring." is ungrammatical. A silly choice should still be a correct sentence.
- B: Same flag on the quiz: only the meaning of the wrong choice should be wrong, not its grammar. The /aʊ/ words crowd/growl/howl/power are correct. It is good that "growl" ties into the story.
- C: Easy to read. "Awooo!" and "Grrr!" are fun. The block-wide TTS blend issue applies here too ("h, ow, l").
- Decision: seg18 say/subtitleJa/subtitleEn -> "Mochi is a hot spring." (A+B). seg0 subtitleJa -> やあ、みんな！ (objective).

#### L225 Welcome to Japan!
- A: The tour-guide review is natural ("Camera on!", "This way, please!"). seg8 "You can tells what to do." sounds like a grammar error when spoken. The wrap "You can welcome friends, say what we have, and what they can do in Japan." is not parallel.
- B: "You can tells" teaches the wrong form right before L229 teaches the s rule. The wrap sentence is 14 words long. The ou/ow lesson and the house/snow quiz are correct.
- C: seg8 is confusing aloud, because "We have tells", "You can tells" and "It's tells" sound like errors. The wrap runs on.
- Decision: seg8 -> `Use "We have" for things in Japan. Use "You can" for things to do. Use "It's" to say how it is.` (A+C). seg20 -> "You can welcome friends to Japan. You can say what we have. You can say what to do. And you can read O U and O W, too!" (B+C).

#### L226 Who Is Your Hero?
- A: The campfire hero talk works ("Cool! My hero is my grandmother.", "My hero is... me!"). "Like Oi! Over here!" is a British (and rude) call, not US English. "Super heroes!" should be one word. The wrap "ask Who is your hero, and answer My hero is." needs quotes. "a camp on the road" is slightly odd but understandable (kept).
- B: who = people is taught clearly, and the Why? quiz distractor is good. The oi tip (おーい) works by itself in Japanese. Conversation line 3 "My hero is ... me!" has a space before the ellipsis, and seg7 writes it without one.
- C: "Oi!" may be read oddly by TTS. The unquoted wrap chain is hard to phrase.
- Decision: seg9 say/subtitleJa/subtitleEn drop "Like Oi! Over here!" (A+C). seg13 "Superheroes!" (objective). seg20 quoted -> `Now you can ask "Who is your hero?" And you can answer "My hero is..."` (A+C). Line 3 + seg7 slide -> "My hero is... me!" (objective). seg0 subtitleJa -> やあ、みんな！ (objective).

#### L227 She Is Brave

#### L228 He Is Good at Soccer
- A: Field talk is natural. "What is your hero good at?" (aiTask and seg17) would be "What's ..." in real speech (lint info). seg8 "Is good at, then the thing. Can, then the action." is not English.
- B: Two patterns (is good at + noun, can + verb) are compared clearly. The tip "おわりは oy、なかは oi" is a correct rule of thumb. "What is" is not a target expression, so a contraction is fine.
- C: The seg8 fragments are hard to read. "Don't forget is." is slightly odd but OK with stress (kept).
- Decision: aiTask starter + seg17 say/subtitleEn -> "What's your hero good at?" (A+C). seg8 -> `"Is good at" plus a thing. ... "Can" plus an action.` (A+C). seg0 subtitleJa -> やあ、みんな！ (objective).

#### L229 He Helps People
- A: The reasons are natural ("She helps sick people.", "I help eat the snacks!"). In the speed challenge, a shouted "Help!" means "I need help", not "help your friend". The wrap "...is your hero with helps" is awkward.
- B: The s rule is clear and links to works. cowboy = cow + boy is a great bridge from ow to oy. The JA たすけて in seg13 also means "help me", which does not match the TPR meaning.
- C: The wrap "with helps" is hard to parse aloud. "Yee-haw!" is fun.
- Decision: seg13 "Help!" -> "Help a friend!" + JA たすけおこして (A+B). seg20 -> `Now you can say why someone is your hero. Use "helps"!` (A+C).

#### L230 This Is My Hero
- A: The poster show is natural. Birimo's speech says "He is kind. He is good at cooking." and then "It's the lunch lady!", which is a contradiction. "Show stars!" reads like a command. TPR "Give a big hand." lacks an object (US: "Give them a big hand"), but it is an item ID and is understood (kept).
- B: Same flag on the he/lunch lady contradiction, because this lesson teaches he/she. The speech order (hero, job, what they're like, what they can do) is clear. seg1 has an 11-word list sentence, which is acceptable (kept).
- C: "Show stars!" sounds odd. "Stars of the show!" has a better rhythm.
- Decision: seg7 say/subtitleEn -> "It's our school cook!" + JA きゅうしょくの おじさん (objective). seg13 -> "Stars of the show!" (A+C).

#### For the coordinator
- **Capital letter after a comma before a TPR command**: "Now, Open your arms wide.", "Then, Eat noodles...", "At the temple, Put your hands together." This is in every L221-230 seg11/seg12 (say + subtitleEn, about 40 fields), and Grep finds about 1,300 "Now, X" hits across worlds 7-40 (0 in worlds 1-6). It is a capitalization error (the command is not a quotation), but it is one mechanical pattern, so I did NOT patch it here. I recommend one block-wide lowercase pass.
- **"みんな やあ！" (space, no comma)** was missed by the やあ、みんな！ rule. I fixed the 4 cases in world 8 (L222, L224, L226, L228). Check the other worlds for the same variant.
- **"X, then the thing / then an action word" grammar explanations** appear in seg8 of many lessons (worlds 7-40). I quoted the frame with "plus" in L221/L222/L228. Other blocks may want the same fix.
- Block-wide TTS risk in letter-by-letter blends ("c, ow, cow", "h, ow, l") is the same as noted for L141-150.
- Kept on purpose: L224 "Very, very yummy!" (natural child talk), "Delicious work!" / "Yummy job!" (playful), L221 "We have many hot springs." (expression ID), L226 "a camp on the road", L228 "Don't forget is.", L230 TPR "Give a big hand." (ID; US would add "them"), L230 seg1 11-word list, and the unquoted "Haru says, In Japan, ..." style.
