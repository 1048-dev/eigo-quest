### Run 9 review notes: L201-210 (world 7, Stage 1)

These notes cover school expressions and grammar, vocab, TPR, phonics words and tips, conversation lines and aiTask, recap, teacherScript, and every teacher segment (say, subtitleJa, subtitleEn, slide). I did not redo or undo the block-wide fixes (double end marks, quiz "A / B" subtitles, "A? Or b?" spoken choices, timetable -> schedule, "Give a thumbs-up.", US praise, showtime, やあ、みんな！). There are 27 patches in 6 lessons (L204, L205, L206, L207, L209, L210). L201, L202, L203 and L208 have no changes. No learning item IDs change (no vocab, expression, TPR or phonics text was touched). One conversation line changes (L205 line 4), and its slide copy was updated with it. New sentences stay at 8 words or fewer, except the wrap list in L210 seg20, which keeps the original length. The check script prints ALL OK: the touched lessons are 203-224 s long with 82-84% English.

#### L201 Jobs in Town
- A: The he/she job talk is natural ("Who is he?", "He is brave.", "Hey, that's my job!"). TPR "Listen to your heart." is also an idiom ("follow your feelings"), but the hand-on-chest action makes the literal meaning clear.
- B: The level is right, and "a before the job" is a clear rule. EN and JA match. The tip's note that cow-ow comes in World 8 is a good forward pointer.
- C: Easy to read. "Ba-bump!" works. The "Sn, ow" blends have the block-wide TTS letter-name risk.
- Decision: no change. "Listen to your heart." is kept (A only, item ID).

#### L202 She Is a Baker
- A: "She can make bread well." is the Japanese-textbook "can ~ well" frame. A US speaker would more often say "She's good at making bread," but the line is grammatical. TPR "Knead the bread." is loose, because you knead dough, but people say it casually.
- B: The job + can link is clear, and the JA きじ fits "knead". "dough" would be a hard spelling at A1. The phonics word "row" is fine here as /roʊ/.
- C: Reads well ("Push, fold, push, fold." and "grow, grow, grow!" have good rhythm). The placeholder "She can... well." is a little awkward aloud, but it is a pattern prompt.
- Decision: no change. "Knead the bread." is kept (A only, item ID).

#### L203 She Works at a Hospital
- A: The town-map dialogue is natural ("She works at a school! Our school!"). "The big red trucks sleep here!" is a cute teacher line.
- B: The third-person s rule is taught clearly ("I work, but he works"). The two-syllable ow words are well chosen. testPoints[1] "ow（2おんせつ）" is terse but understandable.
- C: Reads well. "Yel, low / Pil, low / Sha, dow" are easy to say.
- Decision: no change.

#### L204 Who Is This?
- A: The quiz-show dialogue is natural. In seg8, "The hint uses works at, or can." is stiff. In the seg20 wrap, "You can ask Who is this?, ask for a hint, ..." has broken punctuation.
- B: testPoints[1] "ow の ことばを よみわけられる": every ow word in this lesson says /oʊ/, so there is nothing to tell apart. The seg8 point is fine in JA.
- C: In seg8, the comma in "works at, or can" puts a pause in the wrong place. In seg20, "Who is this?," makes TTS read a question in the middle of a sentence.
- Decision: seg8 say/subtitleEn -> `Give hints with "works at" or "can."` (A+C). seg20 say/subtitleEn -> `You can ask, "Who is this?" You can ask for hints and guess jobs.` (A+C). testPoints[1] -> "ow の ことばを よめる" (objective+B).

#### L205 Town Interview
- A: "What do you do?" and "Thank you for your time." are what a real interviewer says. Haru's "OK, class newspaper." sounds like he is talking to the newspaper.
- B: Ask/answer/tell (you / I / she) is a clear summary, and the oa-middle / ow-end tip is correct. recap[1] "He / She is a ～. works at ～." leaves out the subject of the second pattern.
- C: "OK, class newspaper." is hard to phrase aloud; it is not clear who is being addressed. The rest reads well ("like a turtle").
- Decision: conversation line 4 en/ja and seg7 slide -> "OK, I'll write it down. ..." / 「よし、かいて おくね。…」 (A+C; seg7 say already says "Haru writes it down"). recap[1] -> "He / She is a ～. He / She works at ～. で しょうかい" (objective+B).

#### L206 My Self-Introduction
- A: The speech lines are natural, and "Thank you for listening!" is the right US closer. In seg1, "Name, birthday, likes, and can." uses "can" as a noun, and no teacher talks like that. In seg8, "Start with My name is." needs quotes.
- B: The four-part speech is a good review, and the nickname / pretend-date option protects privacy. EN and JA match. The phonics tip is correct.
- C: The seg1 list ends on a bare "can", which sounds like the word for a tin can. In seg8, the unquoted "Start with My name is. End with Thank you..." gets misphrased.
- Decision: seg1 say/subtitleEn -> "Name, birthday, likes, and what you can do." (A+C). seg8 say/subtitleEn -> quoted phrases (A+C).

#### L207 My Week at School
- A: The ferry dialogue is natural ("We do experiments!", "I'm good at reading."). "Point to Tuesday on your schedule." is fine after the block fix.
- B: The seg1 goal is one 15-word sentence, which is far over Stage 1. The "I have + subject + on + day" order point is clear. The ea/oa contrast is correct.
- C: The seg1 goal cannot be said in one breath. "T, oa, s, t" has the block-wide TTS risk.
- Decision: seg1 say/subtitleEn -> "Our goal: talk about your school week. What's your favorite subject? What are you good at?" (B+C; the JA still matches). The seg20 wrap list is kept, the same as the other wraps.

#### L208 Island Quiz Show
- A: Haru's answer "I know! This is Ms. Emma!" would be "It's Ms. Emma!" in real speech. But "This is ～." is the lesson's target pattern (expressions[1]), so it stays. "Ding, ding, ding! You win!" is natural.
- B: Using "her" with "Her birthday" is clear. seg8 EN says "He for a boy, she for a girl", while JA says おとこの ひと / おんなの ひと. That is a small difference, and both are true.
- C: Reads well. "A queen is a fish. Shake your head!" is fun.
- Decision: no change.

#### L209 Speech Rehearsal
- A: In the aiTask, "Please say your speech." sounds like an order from a coach, and you "give" a speech. In seg8 and the seg20 wrap, the unquoted phrases ("Speak louder, please is a kind way to help.") are hard to follow.
- B: The please-command flag on the aiTask is the same one the lint raised. teacherScript "あしたは はっぴょうかい" assumes the next session is tomorrow (compare the L150 fix). In seg0, "The big show is coming, so today is our rehearsal." has 10 words.
- C: The seg8 and seg20 comma chains are unreadable aloud. The seg0 "so" clause runs on.
- Decision: aiTask.starterEn -> "Can you give your speech?" (A+B). teacherScript あしたは -> つぎは (objective+B). seg0 split into two sentences (B+C). seg8 -> `"Speak louder, please" is a kind way to ask. "You can do it!" is a cheer.` (A+C). seg20 -> `We practiced together. You can ask kindly: "Speak louder, please." You can cheer: "You can do it!"` (A+C). These were changed in both say and subtitleEn.

#### L210 World 7 Grand Review
- A: The show lines are natural. In seg8, "Use I for you" is confusing. In the seg20 wrap, "introduce ... your school week, and town jobs" is a calque of しょうかい, and "find the way" doesn't describe World 8 (giving directions).
- B: The same flags. "I for you" contradicts the JA じぶんは I for a learner, and みちあんない means giving directions. teacherScript "あしたは ボスせん" has the same problem as the L150/L209 fix.
- C: "Use I for you, and he or she for friends." is hard to stress; two short sentences are better.
- Decision: seg8 -> "Use I for yourself. Use he or she for friends." (A+B+C). seg20 -> "You can talk about yourself, ..." and "we'll give directions, ..." (A+B). teacherScript あしたは -> つぎは (objective+B). All were changed in both say and subtitleEn.
